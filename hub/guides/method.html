The problem

Your money is not standing still.

While it sits in a savings account, inflation is quietly doing its work. At 3% per year, the real value of your money shrinks every single month.

Most people respond to this in one of two ways. Neither works.

📊The passive default
Everything into a broad index tracker and hope for the best. Not a bad instinct, but rarely a deliberate one. Most people land there because no one showed them a better-considered alternative, not because they weighed it against one. You get the market return. Nothing more.
Not a strategy
📱Chasing the noise
Whatever name is trending this month. A tip from a podcast, a thread online, a headline about whatever the market has decided to be excited about this week. These stories tend to peak before most people hear about them. By the time something is obvious, it is usually priced in.
Not a strategy either
The method behind TEI sits between the two -- concentrated enough to matter, disciplined enough to survive contact with a real market cycle.
The philosophy

Invest in tomorrow.
Not today.

This method was refined over years of expensive mistakes -- over-investing when the timing was wrong, sitting out when conviction should have meant buying, chasing stories that had already peaked. TEI exists so you do not have to learn those lessons the hard way.

The real mispricing does not live in the names everyone already owns. It lives in structural shifts the market has not yet fully priced, because the payoff is years away, not months. That is where patient, disciplined capital compounds into something that justifies the process.

What earns a name a place: a strong story with structural tailwinds, numbers that confirm the thesis, and leadership worth backing. What does not: excitement, momentum, or the feeling that everyone else is already in.

The TEI Method

The framework

Three steps. Every decision.

Every position in a TEI portfolio is chosen and managed through the same process. No exceptions. Tap each step to explore.

Thematic Conviction
Identify a genuine structural shift big enough to matter over years, not weeks.
What will the world need, use, or rely on in five to ten years that it does not fully price in today? The starting point is always the theme -- autonomous systems, AI infrastructure, space connectivity, the electrification of industry. The theme has to be real, not just a narrative. If the world genuinely moves in this direction, which companies sit at the centre of it?

2
Sized by Risk
Conviction alone does not earn a position its size. Risk determines weight.
A pre-revenue company with a transformational product gets a small position -- the story may be right but the execution risk is high. An established business with a proven model, strong balance sheet, and accelerating numbers gets a larger one. Size is determined by how established the story is, not how exciting it feels.

In the current fund: Nebius is the largest position at 22%, an established AI infrastructure business with a proven model. Kraken Robotics sits at 5%, the same high conviction but early-stage execution risk keeps the position deliberately small until the story matures.
+
3
Timed by Catalysts
Entries and additions are sequenced around real, verifiable events.
Earnings releases, contract wins, product milestones, regulatory approvals -- rather than a fixed calendar or a reaction to headline noise. Buying ahead of a catalyst that confirms the thesis is how the timing edge is generated. Patience in entry is as important as conviction in the idea.

In the current fund: Ondas is held at high conviction despite low current market favour -- the position is being built quietly at a discount, ahead of the catalysts that would confirm the thesis, rather than chasing the name once favour (and the price) has already moved.

The rotation principle -- where returns compound

The largest gains do not come from finding a name and holding it forever. They come from identifying a high-conviction idea while it is still in the top-left quadrant and having the patience to hold through to the top-right. Conviction stays constant. Market favour is what moves -- and that move is where the return lives.

Palantir (PLTR)
For years it sat unloved -- government contracts, unconventional structure, a market that did not yet understand the Ontology thesis. High conviction, low market favour. The investors who built positions quietly during that period and held through the noise captured the full rerating when the market finally caught up.
Alphabet / Google (GOOGL)
Earlier this year the market decided AI would kill search. Sentiment collapsed and the narrative around lost market share dominated. But the numbers never broke. Revenue stayed strong, search remained dominant, and the AI threat proved vastly overstated. Anyone who held conviction through that low-favour period captured one of the fastest recoveries of the year.

Portfolio structure

Two sleeves. Two jobs.

Every TEI portfolio is built from a Growth sleeve and a Foundation sleeve working together. The exact weightings are tailored to individual circumstances -- what matters here is the structure and the thinking behind it.

Growth Sleeve
The engine
70%
Anchor
~50% of Growth
Highest conviction, largest positions. Names where the thesis is well established, numbers are improving, and market favour is high or building. Sized to actually move the needle.
Core
~30% of Growth
Strong conviction, medium positions. Established stories with clear catalysts ahead. Sized to grow into Anchor positions as the thesis matures and market favour builds.
Tail
~20% of Growth
Earlier stage, smaller positions. Higher execution risk but asymmetric upside if the thesis plays out. Sized small enough that a full loss does not damage the portfolio, but large enough to matter if they deliver. Only introduced at full portfolio size.
Foundation Sleeve
The cushion
30%
Developed Indices
~50% of Foundation
Broad exposure to established developed markets. Provides steady compounding and geographic diversification. The bedrock of the Foundation sleeve.
Emerging Markets
~25% of Foundation
Exposure to high-growth economies with a longer time horizon. Higher volatility than developed indices but provides diversification beyond Western markets.
Strategic Equity
~25% of Foundation
Established, more predictable equity holdings selected for resilience. Not exciting -- deliberately so. Its job is to hold steady when the Growth sleeve is under pressure.

On position sizing

Spreading money across too many names too early is the mistake almost everyone makes. Individual ideas can deliver strong percentage returns and the portfolio barely moves -- because each position was too small to matter. A portfolio with eight well-sized positions will almost always outperform one with twenty-five under-sized ones.

Cash and dip buying

Cash is not dead weight.
It is optionality.

Most investors treat cash as something to be deployed as quickly as possible -- almost an embarrassment, money sitting idle when it could be working. The TEI approach is different.

A cash allocation of 5-10% (adjusted for market conditions and individual circumstances) exists for one specific purpose: to act decisively when conviction names pull back hard.

Cash allocation range
0%5-10% target100%

The key distinction is between volatility and thesis failure. They look similar in the moment. They require completely different responses.

Volatility
Market sold off, sector rotated, short-term miss. Thesis unchanged. Price has changed. This is the opportunity.
Thesis failure
Contract lost, competitor emerged, management pivoted. The investment case has changed. This is an exit, not a dip.
Response framework
Select a market scenario
Red day Down 5-10%
Nibble -- add a small number of shares in names you already hold conviction in, if there is cash available to deploy. Do not force it if no attractive entry exists. This is a gentle response to a modest move.
Heavy red day Down 10-20%
Add more meaningfully -- a larger deployment into your highest-conviction names, provided the story has not changed. Most heavy red days are followed by a relief bounce. Prices tend to be higher sooner than it feels possible in the moment.
Macro-driven fall Sustained pressure
Scale up further in proportion to the size of the move. Recent tariff and geopolitical scares are good examples -- markets fell sharply, bottomed, bounced, and within 10-20 trading days had pushed on to new highs. The discipline is staying invested and deploying into the fear.

Before adding on any fall: confirm the thesis still holds. A lower price is not automatically an opportunity. The question is always whether the story has changed -- not just whether the number has.

Building the machine

The first 18 months.

It takes roughly 12 to 18 months to build a portfolio to the point where it becomes a well-oiled machine. Before that point, the job is not to chase returns -- it is to build the foundations. Tap each stage to explore.

Months 0-6
Build the foundations
The job is not to generate returns -- it is to build each position up to a size where it can actually move the needle. Get the right tax wrappers in place. Make the first contributions into your highest-conviction Anchor names. Resist the urge to spread across too many names too early.
Months 6-12
Build the habit
Regular contributions into the highest-conviction names. Positions built deliberately to a meaningful size. Catalysts tracked, not guessed at. The portfolio will likely run red at points -- that is not a sign something has gone wrong. It is the normal texture of building properly rather than chasing a quick win.
Months 12-18
The machine takes shape
Positions start reaching meaningful sizes. The two-sleeve structure begins to function as designed -- Growth providing the upside, Foundation providing the cushion. Volatility starts to feel like information rather than a threat. The compounding clock is running.
18 months and beyond
Patience becomes the edge
Most retail investors either overtrade -- reacting to every piece of news -- or freeze, waiting for certainty that never arrives. The investors who built real wealth in this framework did neither. They stayed systematic, treated discomfort as signal noise, and let compounding do its work. That patience is a competitive advantage almost no one has the discipline to sustain.

Give the process time. Stay invested with money you can afford to leave alone for at least three years.

Learn to sit with discomfort -- because the best returns come to the investors who could tolerate the drawdowns that scared everyone else out.

What Is Agility Robotics?

Agility Robotics (founded 2015, Salem, Oregon) is the only humanoid robotics company in the world with multiple years of verified commercial deployment revenue. Its flagship product, Digit, is a bipedal humanoid robot purpose-built to work in environments designed for people - navigating doorways, stairs, ramps, and warehouse aisles without facility modification. It lifts up to 35 pounds, operates for up to 16 hours on a charge, and handles repetitive physical tasks like tote transfer, machine tending, and material sortation.

The company was spun out of Oregon State University's Dynamic Robotics Laboratory by co-founders Dr Jonathan Hurst (Chief Robot Officer), Dr Damion Shelton, and Mikhail Jones. CEO Peggy Johnson is a former Microsoft and Magic Leap executive. The company operates RoboFab - the world's first dedicated humanoid robot factory, 70,000 square feet in Salem, Oregon, with theoretical production capacity of 10,000+ units per year.

The pitch in one sentence: Agility is going public at $2.5 billion when every comparable private-market humanoid company is valued at $5B–$39B - despite being the only one with verified, multi-year commercial deployments in production environments. It is not the most funded. It is the most deployed.

Digit v5 is not an incremental upgrade - it is the product that unlocks the market. As co-founder Dr Jonathan Hurst stated: “Safety is the biggest blocker to adoption. Agility Arc - The Software Layer

Agility Arc is the cloud-based fleet management platform that ships with every Digit deployment. It handles task assignment, deployment coordination, and integration with enterprise systems (WMS, WES, MES), enabling deployment in hours or days rather than weeks. Arc converts Agility from a hardware company into a software-and-services platform - and is currently offered free to customers. Its future monetisation as a separate revenue stream is not in any current analyst model. This is the hidden margin expansion lever.

SPAC dilution is the key structural risk. The sponsor promote dilutes existing CCXI shareholders at close. Potential PIPE dilution ($200M at $10 with lockup) creates paper overhang. If redemptions are high before the vote (shareholders can redeem at trust value), the $420M trust could shrink significantly. The S-4 filing - expected within 2–3 months of the announcement - will contain full audited financials and disclose current revenue for the first time. This is the most important document in the AGLT investment case.

Business Model: Robots-as-a-Service

Agility operates a dual model - direct robot sales and a subscription-based RaaS offering - but has been deliberately shifting toward RaaS as its dominant go-to-market strategy. RaaS is the more interesting business model structurally: it creates recurring revenue, reduces customer adoption barriers, retains robot ownership (enabling data collection and OTA improvements), and creates the kind of valuation profile that public markets reward with software-company multiples rather than hardware multiples.

The GXO relationship - 100,000+ totes, multi-year RaaS - is a reference sale that Figure AI does not have yet.

The Bull Case

The access trade: AGLT will be, on listing, the only way for a US retail or institutional investor to own a pure-play humanoid robotics company through a standard brokerage account. That structural demand is material - humanoid robots are the single most-discussed AI hardware theme of 2026, and until AGLT lists, there is no direct US equity. In the immediate post-listing period, category demand alone could sustain a premium to the $2.5B deal value.

The deployment moat: 65,000+ hours of verified commercial operation is a genuine competitive advantage. Each hour generates proprietary embodied AI training data that Agility is compounding through its software stack. Competitors starting from zero operational hours are not just behind on hardware - they are behind on the training data that makes the AI layer genuinely useful in production environments.

The NVIDIA Halos unlock: Agility was announced June 22 as the first commercial humanoid to adopt NVIDIA's Halos safety architecture - two days before the CCXI merger announcement. This is not a coincidence. Halos enables Digit v5 to operate without safety barriers, which is the product-market fit event for scaled commercial deployment. NVIDIA's endorsement is strategic validation of the highest order, comparable in significance to NVIDIA's $2B investment in Lumentum for the photonics thesis.

The Amazon dormant anchor: Amazon invested $150M in Agility's Series B and has run multiple Digit pilots. The company has explicitly confirmed it is waiting to restart Amazon deployments with Digit v5. If Amazon activates as an active deployment customer post-Digit v5 launch, the revenue ramp is potentially enormous - Amazon operates hundreds of fulfilment and sortation centres globally. This single catalyst could justify the entire current valuation on its own.

The valuation discount: At $2.5B, Agility is priced at a fraction of its nearest comparable. Figure AI is valued at $39B - 15x Agility - without a single commercial humanoid deployment verified at GXO's scale. Apptronik is at $5B with $935M raised. Boston Dynamics is not accessible publicly. The public-market entry point for humanoid robotics has rarely been this early or this discounted relative to private-market peers.

Risks: Clear-Eyed

No disclosed revenue SPAC dilution structure Digit v5 timeline risk Better-funded competition $300M order milestone-dependent Single-site deployments only

Zero disclosed revenue. Agility chose not to disclose revenue in the Investor Presentation filed with the June 24 CCXI announcement - an unusual decision that raised questions across the analyst community. The S-4, expected 2–3 months post-announcement, will be the first public disclosure. Cash burn was approximately $100M in 2025. At $620M in gross proceeds and current burn rate, the runway extends roughly 6 years - but the question of when revenue becomes self-sustaining is unanswered.

SPAC structural risks. SPAC mergers have a mixed track record. Sponsor promote dilution is real. High redemptions before the vote could reduce trust proceeds significantly below $420M. Lock-up expiry - 180 days post-close for existing shareholders - creates potential selling pressure in mid-2027. The de-SPAC listing premium has historically compressed 12–18 months post-close as hype fades and fundamentals matter more.

Digit v5 timeline has already slipped. The “cooperatively safe Digit” was originally targeted “within 18 months” of late 2024 - implying mid-2026. The current published timeline is early 2027. A second slip would delay the core scaling event and raise questions about execution discipline.

Competition is better-funded and accelerating. Figure AI is producing 1 robot/hour, has $39B valuation, and OpenAI partnership. Unitree shipped 5,500+ units in 2025 at 1/10th the price of Digit. Chinese competitors have 70% of global robotics component supply chains and can undercut on hardware cost. Tesla's automotive supply chain advantages are formidable at scale. If any of these achieve a breakthrough in cooperative safety ahead of Digit v5's launch, the first-mover moat erodes quickly.

The $300M order needs context. The “$300M+ in contracted orders” headline refers to a single 3-year RaaS contract for 1,000 Digit v5 units from one undisclosed customer, subject to milestone achievement. It is not revenue. It is not a purchase order. It depends on Digit v5 shipping on schedule, the customer accepting the milestone deliverables, and Agility executing at production scale it has never achieved. The GeekWire and New Market Pitch analyses are essential reading on this specific point before drawing conclusions from the headline number.

This is a pre-revenue, pre-profit, SPAC-structured investment in a competitive market with better-funded private rivals. Agility is the first to market but not necessarily the best positioned to win at scale. Position sizing should reflect that this is an early-stage bet on a category thesis, not a validated business model. The S-4 filing and Digit v5 launch are the two events that will determine whether the thesis accelerates or requires re-evaluation.

Bottom Line: The Category Trade, at the Category Price

Agility Robotics is not the most funded, not the most advanced, and not the most ambitious humanoid company. It is the most commercially deployed - and the only one going public in 2026 in the United States. That combination is genuinely rare.

The structural case for CCXI/AGLT is not primarily about Agility's current financial metrics (which are undisclosed). It is about owning the only US-listed pure-play access point to a $1 trillion TAM at the moment the category is graduating from prototype to commercial reality - and doing so at a valuation ($2.5B) that is a fraction of private-market comparables ($39B Figure AI, $5B Apptronik) before a single dollar of public-market revenue has been disclosed.

The honest risk framing: You are buying a story. @TacticzH is right that the stock at $17 (post-announcement) is not an easy buy - it's a 70% premium to trust value before the S-4, before revenue disclosure, before Digit v5 launches. The SPAC structure adds complexity. Competition is accelerating. Timeline slippage has happened once already.

For growth fund positioning: CCXI/AGLT is a speculative, category-access position - sized small, held with a 3–5 year minimum horizon, and reviewed sharply at two events: the S-4 filing (revenue reality check) and the Digit v5 launch (scaling gate). If both clear positively, the thesis reaccelerates. If either disappoints, the position should be reassessed. @crux_capital_'s 18-point framework on Substack is the most comprehensive due diligence tool available in the public domain before the S-4 arrives - required reading before sizing any position.

The long-term frame: In a market where the humanoid robotics category is worth $1 trillion, does the first US-listed pure-play company - with 65,000+ verified commercial hours, NVIDIA's safety architecture, Amazon as a waiting deployment customer, and $620M in fresh capital - deserve to be valued at $2.5B? History suggests categories reward their first public movers disproportionately, even when better-funded private competitors exist. AGLT is the bet that history repeats.

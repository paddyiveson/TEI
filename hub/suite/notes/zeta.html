Start Here: What Does Zeta Global Actually Do?

Zeta Global is a technology company that helps the world's biggest brands figure out who to market to, what to say, and exactly when to say it - using artificial intelligence and one of the largest consumer databases ever assembled. If you've ever received a well-timed email about something you were genuinely thinking about buying, or seen an ad on a website that felt uncannily relevant, there's a reasonable chance Zeta's technology was involved.

The company was founded in 2007 by David Steinberg - a serial entrepreneur - and John Sculley, the former CEO of Apple who led the company through its mid-1980s growth years. Steinberg remains CEO today and has been the driving force behind an eighteen-year-long bet on data and artificial intelligence at a time when most marketing tech companies were focused on simpler tools.

The Problem

Why Is Marketing So Broken - and Why Does It Stay That Way?

Imagine you run a large bank. You want to find new customers for your credit card. To do that, you probably use five or six different tools: one to email existing customers, a different one to place ads on Google, another for Facebook, a separate system for television advertising, and yet another for text messages. Each of these tools holds a different piece of information about your customers. None of them talk to each other. Your marketing team spends half their time just trying to stitch data together before they can do anything useful with it.

Now multiply this across your entire marketing budget, add in the fact that the old system of tracking people across the internet (via third-party cookies) is collapsing, and layer on the reality that your competitors are doing exactly the same thing. What you end up with is an enormous amount of marketing spend that doesn't work very well - with no single place to see whether any of it is actually driving results.

This is the state of enterprise marketing today. The tools are fragmented. The data is siloed. The intelligence is limited. And the waste is enormous - analysts estimate that up to 40% of global marketing spend generates no measurable return. At global digital ad spend of over $600 billion annually, that's hundreds of billions of pounds and dollars spent on marketing that misses.

Think of it this way: Legacy marketing tech is like trying to run a large restaurant kitchen where the chef, the sous-chef, the pastry chef, and the bar all work from different recipe books, can't communicate with each other, and each have their own separate ingredient store. Zeta is the unified operating system that puts one kitchen, one recipe book, and one ingredient store in its place - and then adds a head chef who never sleeps and gets smarter with every meal served.

The Solution

What Zeta Builds: The AI Marketing Platform

Zeta's answer to this problem is the Zeta Marketing Platform (ZMP) - a single unified system that combines three things that every brand needs but currently has to buy from multiple different vendors:

Identity: Knowing who your customers and prospects actually are, across every device and channel, as a persistent real-world person rather than a series of fragmented digital signals. Zeta achieves this through its SuperGraph - a proprietary identity engine covering over 245 million US consumers with more than 2,500 attributes per person. This is the crown jewel of the business. No other independent marketing platform has a comparable owned dataset of this scale and quality.

Intelligence: Knowing what those people are likely to do next. Zeta's AI engine processes more than one trillion consumer signals per month - things like email opens, website visits, purchase intent signals, actual transaction data, and behavioural patterns - to build predictive scores for every individual. These scores tell a brand not just who a customer is, but whether they are about to buy a car, switch insurance providers, or cancel their subscription.

Activation: Actually doing something with that intelligence at scale, across every relevant channel - email, SMS, connected TV (streaming ads), programmatic display, social media, and more - from a single interface with unified measurement. Instead of five vendors with five logins and five separate reporting dashboards, one platform. One view of the customer. One measurement of what works.

Think of it this way: If a major brand's marketing department is like a military operation, legacy tools give them individual soldiers with no communication. Zeta gives them a unified command centre, a live intelligence feed on the enemy (the consumer's likely next move), and the ability to deploy all forces in a coordinated strike - in real time, at scale, with full visibility on outcomes.

The Data Asset

Why 245 Million Consumer Profiles Is Not Just a Number

The SuperGraph is what separates Zeta from every other company in its competitive space. Zeta owns this data - it was not purchased from Google or Facebook, it does not rely on third-party cookies that are being phased out, and it cannot be replicated by a competitor writing a cheque. It was assembled over 18 years from owned and operated web properties (including Disqus, one of the world's largest comment platforms with hundreds of millions of user interactions), an authenticated publisher network (LiveIntent), direct brand data partnerships, and a network of publisher relationships that see permissioned, first-party consumer behaviour at scale.

Why does this matter now more than ever? Because the two mechanisms that brands have traditionally used to find and track consumers online are being dismantled simultaneously. Third-party cookies - the little files websites use to track people across the internet - are being deprecated across all major browsers. Mobile device IDs on Apple and Android devices are being locked down by privacy controls. Brands that relied on these mechanisms to target ads are suddenly finding that their existing tech stacks no longer work the way they used to. They need a new approach - one built on permissioned, first-party, deterministic consumer data. That is exactly what Zeta's SuperGraph provides. The regulatory tailwind is turning Zeta's proprietary data into a competitive moat that only widens as cookie deprecation accelerates.

Pillar 3 in Detail: Why AI Agents Make Zeta More Valuable, Not Less

This is the part of the thesis that most people miss. A common fear about AI is that it will replace the software companies that power existing workflows. For most marketing tech companies, this fear is valid - AI can generate email copy, design ads, and automate campaign management, which threatens companies whose value is purely in those execution layers.

But Zeta is not primarily an execution company. It is a data and intelligence company. And as AI agents proliferate across enterprise marketing - agents that autonomously decide which customers to contact, when, with what message, across which channel - every one of those agents needs an answer to the same foundational question: who exactly should I be talking to right now, and what are they likely to do next?

That answer requires the SuperGraph. It requires the trillion monthly signals. It requires the 18 years of first-party consumer intelligence that Zeta has assembled. An AI agent without that data layer is making random guesses. An AI agent plugged into the ZMP is making decisions with the best consumer intelligence on the planet. The rise of agentic AI in marketing makes Zeta's data asset more mission-critical, not less.

Zeta's own AI agent - Athena - launched in general availability in March 2026 and expanded to advertising agencies at Cannes Lions in June 2026. In its first week of GA availability, Athena drove 7x more agent interactions and accounted for over 60% of all AI platform usage. Athena is positioned not just as a product feature, but as the interface through which AI agents across the marketing ecosystem will interact with Zeta's data and intelligence layer - making it an "operating system for agentic marketing."

The Palantir partnership (June 2026) is the clearest signal of what Zeta is building. Zeta announced a multi-year partnership to rebuild its Data Cloud on Palantir Foundry - the enterprise AI infrastructure platform used by the world's most data-intensive organisations. This combines Palantir's governance, operational tooling, and enterprise AI infrastructure with Zeta's marketing identity and consumer intelligence. The Palantir partnership is expected to generate over $100M in annual revenue in coming years and opens ZETA to an entirely new segment of enterprise customer that previously required Palantir-level data infrastructure to engage. Roth Capital called it a transformational deal and raised its price target to $44.

Financial Performance: The Numbers That Matter

What follows are the key metrics directly from Zeta's earnings presentations - the figures Zeta's management highlight as their core measures of business health and investor evidence.

19 Consecutive Beat-and-Raise Quarters - and What That Actually Means

Every single quarter since Zeta went public, the company has beaten analyst revenue expectations and raised its forward guidance. Not once has it missed and cut. Nineteen quarters in a row. For context: this is two full years beyond what most high-growth software companies manage before a stumble. It reflects a business with high revenue visibility from long-term enterprise contracts, tight execution from management, and a model that generates predictable, recurring cash flows rather than lumpy transactional revenue. Q1 2026 was the largest single-quarter beat in company history - $26 million above guidance midpoint.

Alongside this, Zeta achieved the Rule of 67 in Q1 2026. The Rule of 40 is the standard software industry efficiency benchmark - combining a company's revenue growth rate and profitability margin to produce a single score. Anything above 40 is considered excellent. Zeta scored 67: revenue growth of 50% plus adjusted EBITDA margin of 17%. For a company of its scale, this places Zeta among the elite tier of enterprise software businesses globally. David Steinberg cited this on the Q1 earnings call as evidence that “we are winning in this environment.”

Net Revenue Retention (NRR): The Number That Proves the Product Works

Net Revenue Retention measures how much revenue Zeta earns from its existing customers this year compared to what those same customers spent last year. A score of 100% means customers are spending exactly the same amount - flat. A score above 100% means they are spending more, even before a single new customer is signed.

Zeta's NRR hit a record high of 120% in 2025, up from 114% in 2024 and 113% in 2021. In Q1 2026, net retention remained above the company's target range of 110–115%. What this means in practice: even if Zeta never signed another new customer, revenue would continue growing by 20% per year just from existing ones spending more. This is the clearest possible evidence that the ZMP is delivering measurable ROI for clients - because enterprises do not voluntarily increase their software spend without a business justification. The NRR is also supported by platform depth metrics from Q1 2026: super-scaled customers using more than one use case were up over 50% year-on-year, and customers using more than three channels were up 40% year-on-year - meaning customers are expanding across the platform, not just spending more on what they already use.

Why NRR above 115% is elite: Best-in-class SaaS businesses like Snowflake, Datadog, and Palantir have historically achieved NRR of 120–130% at their peak growth phases. An NRR of 120% at Zeta's $1.3B revenue base means approximately $260M in annual revenue growth from existing customers alone - before Zeta acquires a single new logo. The land-and-expand model is working exactly as designed.

The Undervaluation Case: Why the Market Is Getting This Wrong

At approximately $20 per share and a market cap of roughly $6 billion, ZETA trades at around 3.4x its 2026 guided revenue of $1.785 billion. For a software company growing revenue at 37% year-over-year, generating positive free cash flow, and guiding to its first year of positive GAAP net income, that multiple is strikingly cheap.

The Miscast Problem

Bank of America's analyst Matt Bullock captured the core issue precisely when he reinstated coverage in 2026: investors have “miscast and mispriced Zeta as a cyclical digital advertising play.” The market is valuing ZETA as though it were a company like The Trade Desk - a business whose revenue moves up and down with the broader advertising cycle, driven by media budget decisions. But Zeta's business is structurally different. The majority of its revenue comes from long-term platform contracts with enterprises that have deeply embedded ZMP into their marketing operations. These are not discretionary advertising buys - they are infrastructure decisions, with multi-year lock-in and high switching costs.

Risks: What Could Go Wrong

Short seller history Privacy regulation Salesforce/Adobe competition Marigold integration Valuation re-rating not guaranteed Insider selling

Short seller overhang from 2024. Fuzzy Panda Research published a short report in October 2024 alleging revenue inflation and questionable customer metrics. Zeta denied all allegations and has since delivered three consecutive quarters of accelerating growth that effectively rebuts the core claims - but the report contributed to a significant stock decline from which the stock is still recovering. The short interest and associated sceptical institutional sentiment continues to create a ceiling on the stock that will only lift as fundamental performance compounds.

Privacy regulation is a double-edged sword. While the deprecation of third-party cookies is a tailwind for Zeta's first-party data advantage, an acceleration of privacy regulation could also constrain how Zeta itself collects and uses consumer signals. If regulators move to restrict even permissioned first-party data collection, the SuperGraph's continued expansion could be challenged. The company's compliance architecture is designed for this environment, but regulatory risk cannot be dismissed.

Salesforce and Adobe are not standing still. Both companies have announced major AI investments (Salesforce Agentforce, Adobe GenStudio) and have significantly larger enterprise sales forces, brand recognition, and customer relationship depth than Zeta. If either executes their AI platform vision effectively, the window for Zeta's platform consolidation thesis could narrow. The competitive moat is real, but it is not impenetrable.

Marigold integration complexity. The Marigold acquisition added five enterprise software businesses simultaneously. Integration risk is real - cross-selling, platform unification, and cultural integration of this scope historically creates execution distraction. The revenue contribution is included in guidance, but upside from synergies remains unproven.

The valuation re-rating is not automatic. Zeta can continue executing flawlessly and the market may still treat it as an ad-tech company if institutional perception doesn't shift. The 20x beat-and-raise record has not been enough on its own to close the gap to SaaS multiples - the Palantir partnership and Athena agency expansion are the most credible catalysts for forcing that reclassification. Watch analyst coverage evolution closely through 2026.

Bottom Line: The Fastest-Growing Platform the Market Hasn't Priced as One

Zeta Global is a company delivering 37-50% revenue growth with positive free cash flow, a 19-quarter flawless execution record, a proprietary AI data asset covering 245 million consumers that took 18 years to build, and an AI agent platform that launched to immediate, extraordinary adoption. It is guiding to its first year of positive GAAP net income in 2026. It trades at 3.4x forward revenue - a fraction of the multiple commanded by slower-growing competitors with inferior data assets and no AI agent capability.

The core argument: The market has labelled ZETA as a cyclical ad-tech company and priced it accordingly. The business is actually an enterprise SaaS platform with high switching costs, expanding ARPU, recurring contract revenue, and a data moat that compounds with every customer interaction. When the market reclassifies it as such - catalysed by the Palantir partnership, Athena adoption, and two more quarters of 20%+ organic growth - the valuation gap closes sharply. 16 analysts cover ZETA with an average 12-month target of $28.46 - 58% above current prices. Roth Capital's $44 target (post-Palantir) represents a 120% upside case.

The AI agent angle: As agentic AI transforms enterprise marketing, every marketing AI agent needs the data and identity layer to function. Zeta's SuperGraph is that layer. The rise of AI doesn't threaten Zeta - it makes Zeta's data infrastructure more critical with every agent deployed. This is the multi-year secular upside that current guidance does not yet capture.

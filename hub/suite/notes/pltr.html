Start Here: What You've Heard, and Why It's Wrong

If you've come across Palantir in the news or online, there's a reasonable chance it was in one of three contexts: as a controversial surveillance company connected to Peter Thiel, as a meme stock that went to the moon in 2021 and crashed back to earth in 2022, or as a dry reference to “AI software.” None of those framings tell you what Palantir actually does - or why, as of mid-2026, it is producing results that are almost without precedent in the history of enterprise software.

The name itself comes from J.R.R. Tolkien's Lord of the Rings: the palantíri were magical seeing stones that allowed their holders to perceive distant events and truth through vast amounts of information. That is, deliberately and somewhat immodestly, what the founders believed their software would do: give organisations the ability to see clearly through the noise of complex, fragmented data to make better decisions.

Let's address the elephant in the room

Is Palantir a Surveillance Company?

Palantir builds software for intelligence agencies and defence organisations that, among other things, helps them connect disparate data to identify threats. This has included work for the CIA, NSA, US Army, and allied intelligence services. It has also been used in immigration enforcement contexts that generated significant protest in 2018-2019.

The honest answer is: Palantir's government software can be used for surveillance, in the same way that a hospital database system could be misused. The question of whether you are comfortable with the end uses of that software is a legitimate ethical consideration. This note does not dismiss those concerns - it is for you to weigh them.

What this note will do is explain the actual business, the actual technology, and the actual investment case - which is largely a story about commercial enterprise AI, not government surveillance. Approximately half of Palantir's revenue now comes from private sector companies using its platform to run their operations more efficiently. The surveillance concern, while valid for the government side, does not define what the company has become commercially.

The meme stock question

Wasn't This a Reddit Stock That Crashed?

Yes - and the story of why it crashed, and what happened next, is essential context for understanding the current investment case.

Palantir went public in September 2020 via a direct listing. By early 2021, the stock had risen to approximately $45 per share, driven largely by retail enthusiasm and broader tech/growth stock mania during the pandemic era. Then interest rates rose, growth stocks fell out of favour, and PLTR collapsed all the way to approximately $6 per share by late 2022. It had lost over 85% of its peak value. Many retail investors who bought in 2021 were badly burned. The “meme stock” label came from this episode.

What changed the story was not hype. It was a product: in June 2023, Palantir launched AIP - its Artificial Intelligence Platform. Within a year, commercial revenue had inflected from flat to triple-digit growth rates. By September 2024, the company was added to the S&P 500, triggering billions in mandatory institutional buying. By Q1 2026, the company was growing revenue at 85% year-on-year - its fastest rate ever - with profit margins of 53% on GAAP net income. The stock is now at ~$129, down about 27% from its late-2025 peak of ~$165, not because the business is deteriorating, but because the valuation got ahead of even exceptional fundamentals.

The meme stock era is behind it. What exists now is a company with genuinely extraordinary operating metrics that most investors - including many professionals - still don't fully understand. That is where the opportunity lies.

What Palantir Actually Does: The Simple Version

At its core, Palantir builds software that helps large, complex organisations make better decisions faster, by taking the enormous quantities of data they already hold and turning it into something their people - and now AI agents - can actually use.

To understand why this is so hard and so valuable, you need to understand the problem first.

The Problem

The Data Chaos That Lives Inside Every Large Organisation

Imagine a large manufacturing company. It has a factory management system, a supply chain system, a customer relationship platform, an HR system, a finance system, multiple spreadsheets maintained by individual departments, and machine sensors outputting real-time data from the factory floor. Each of these systems stores data in a completely different format. None of them talk to each other. The factory floor sensor knows a machine is running 12% below capacity. The finance system knows that the contract for that machine's output needs to be fulfilled by Friday. The supply chain system knows the replacement part is sitting in a warehouse two miles away. But because these systems are siloed and incompatible, no single person or system has a clear picture of all three facts at once - which means nobody makes the obvious decision (fetch the part) until it's too late.

Now multiply this across a US Army brigade with 5,000 soldiers, 300 vehicles, and real-time intelligence feeds, all arriving in different formats from different systems. Or an NHS hospital trust managing 5,000 patients, 800 staff, and 200 wards with incompatible scheduling and medical record systems. The problem of fragmented, incompatible, siloed data is not a technology problem - it is a structural problem that exists in every large organisation on the planet, and it gets worse as organisations grow and systems age.

Think of it this way: Traditional enterprise software is like having a brilliant team of specialists who each speak a different language and are locked in separate rooms. The supply chain expert speaks French, the finance team speaks German, the operations team speaks Japanese. Each does great work in their room. But nobody can combine their knowledge into a coordinated decision. Palantir is the universal translator that lets them not just communicate, but act together in real time.

Why start with governments?

The Government Contract as an R&D Programme

Palantir's decision to start with intelligence agencies was not just ideological - it was a business strategy. Governments dealing with national security problems have four characteristics that make them ideal first customers for genuinely hard software: they have the most complex, fragmented data problems of any organisation on earth; they will pay almost anything for software that actually works; they demand the highest possible standards for security, reliability, and accountability; and they cannot fire the vendor easily once the software is embedded in critical operations.

This meant Palantir spent 15 years getting paid to solve the hardest data problems in existence, building the most rigorous security and governance infrastructure of any software company, and accumulating the institutional knowledge of how to integrate incompatible systems at scale. When the commercial market eventually reached the same level of data complexity - and when AI arrived to create demand for a platform that could make AI agents actually useful - Palantir had already solved these problems for a decade. No commercial software company has that background.

The Core Technology - Most Important Section of This Note

The Ontology: Why This Is the Most Important Piece of Software Most Investors Have Never Heard Of

The Ontology is Palantir's most important competitive advantage. It is the reason clients cannot leave, the reason AI agents deployed on Palantir work differently to those deployed anywhere else, and the reason that traditional enterprise software companies - Salesforce, SAP, Oracle - are structurally a decade behind despite having a thousand times more customers. Understanding the Ontology is the key to understanding why Palantir is genuinely n of 1, as CEO Alex Karp describes it.

What Is It, in Plain English?

Every organisation in the world holds data in tables: rows and columns in databases. A table called customer_records_v2 might contain columns like cust_id, acq_date, seg_code. This means nothing to a person who hasn't read the documentation. It means nothing to an AI agent. And it means nothing to an analyst trying to answer a business question like “which of our customers are most likely to churn in the next 30 days?”

The Ontology translates these raw tables into business objects: real-world things that a business actually cares about. Instead of customer_records_v2, you have a Customer object, with properties like Name, AcquisitionDate, Segment, and relationships like hasOrders, isServedBy, livesin. Instead of separate, incompatible database tables, you have a unified, semantically meaningful map of every entity in your business and how they relate to each other - in language that humans and AI agents can both understand and reason over.

Think of it this way: Imagine Google Maps. Before Google Maps, you had road data as raw GPS coordinates: “48.8566° N, 2.3522° E.” Useful to a computer. Useless to a person. Google Maps translated those coordinates into “Paris, Eiffel Tower, 1 km via Rue de Rivoli.” It connected raw data points into a semantic map that humans can navigate and reason about. The Ontology does exactly this for an organisation's data - it creates a navigable, semantically meaningful map of the entire enterprise that both humans and AI agents can explore, query, and act upon.

Nouns, Verbs, and the Kinetic Layer

The genius of the Ontology is that it captures not just what things are - the semantic layer - but what can be done to them - the kinetic layer. Palantir calls these Objects (nouns) and Actions (verbs).

The Nouns - Semantic Layer

Objects, Properties & Links

Every entity in the business: Customers, Aircraft, Purchase Orders, Field Engineers, Factory Machines, Threat Actors. Each with its properties and its live relationships to every other object. A Machine is located_in a Factory, produces a Part, assigned_to an Engineer, has_status of running/maintenance/fault.

Customer → hasOrders → Order → containsProducts → Product

The Verbs - Kinetic Layer

Actions, Logic & Governance

The things you can do to objects, with governance, security, and audit built in. An Action might be “approve this purchase order,” “escalate this alert to the duty manager,” or “reroute this shipment via Hamburg.” Every action has defined permissions (who can trigger it), validation rules (what conditions must be met), and an automatic audit trail (who did what, when, and on what data).

ApprovePurchaseOrder | RequiresApprover | WritesTo:ERP | AuditLogged

The Brain - Logic Layer

Business Rules, Models & AI

The reasoning that determines which action to take. This can be a simple business rule (“escalate if inventory drops below 10 units”), a machine learning model, or an LLM-driven AI agent that reasons in natural language over live Ontology data. All logic is connected to the same Ontology, and therefore operates on the same shared understanding of the business.

if Stock.quantity < 10: trigger ReorderAction | or: AIPAgent.query("which suppliers can deliver fastest?")

The Guardrails - Security Layer

Permissions, Governance & Audit

Every data access, every action, every AI agent interaction is governed by the same security rules, recorded in an immutable audit log, and bounded by the permissions of the requesting user or agent. An AI agent cannot access data it's not authorised to see. An action cannot be taken by an agent that doesn't have the relevant permission. Every decision is attributable. This is what makes enterprise AI deployable in regulated, audited, high-stakes environments.

Agent_Revenue_Analyst: can_read[Customer, Order] cannot_read[Salary, PII] cannot_trigger[ApprovePayment]

Why This Creates an Unbreakable Moat

Building an Ontology is not like installing software. It requires months of work, typically involving Palantir's Forward Deployed Engineers (FDEs) working side by side with the client's team to map every data source, every business process, every decision into the shared semantic model. It involves the client exposing their most sensitive operational data and processes. It involves training hundreds of employees on how to work within the new system. Once complete, the Ontology is not just a database - it is a living institutional memory of how the organisation operates.

The switching cost is extraordinary. Switching from Palantir to a competitor does not mean uninstalling software - it means dismantling your entire operational intelligence infrastructure, losing years of embedded institutional knowledge, retraining your workforce, rebuilding every application and workflow, and doing so while continuing to run a business in real time. Palantir's CTO Shyam Sankar has said: “Our customers don't just use our software. They build their businesses on it.” A data engineer who switched from another stack to Palantir described it as: “I spent two years building data models in Databricks. Two weeks in Ontology and I threw all of that away.” This is not a product. It is an operating system for the enterprise - and operating systems, once established, rarely get replaced.

Why Traditional SaaS Is 10 Years Behind

The most common question about Palantir's competitive position is: “Why can't Salesforce or Oracle just build this?” It is a fair question. Both companies are vastly larger, have more customers, more resources, and more engineers. The answer reveals why software architecture decisions made decades ago create durable, structural advantages that money alone cannot overcome.

✕  How Traditional SaaS Works

Data stored in isolated, system-specific silos (Salesforce knows about customers; SAP knows about supply chains; they don't share a data model)
AI is a feature bolted on top of existing data structures (Salesforce Einstein, Oracle AI) - it reads your CRM data but cannot see or reason over your supply chain
No shared semantic layer - “Customer” means something different in Salesforce vs SAP vs the finance system
Actions are system-specific - approving a purchase order in SAP does not automatically update the Salesforce forecast or the warehouse system
AI cannot take governed, cross-system actions - it can generate text, not change a field in your ERP
Building an Ontology on top of these systems would require rebuilding their entire architecture - billions of dollars and a decade

✓  How Palantir's Ontology Works

All data sources - regardless of format or system - are integrated into one shared semantic model
AI agents (AIP) sit natively on top of the Ontology and have access to the entire enterprise's live data in one coherent model
One shared definition of every business concept - “Customer” means the same thing in the supply chain, the CRM, and the finance model
Actions are cross-system and governed - approve an order in one action, ERP is updated, forecast is recalculated, warehouse system is notified
AI can take real, governed, auditable actions that affect real systems - with full permission controls and audit trail
The Ontology is the architecture - it cannot be replicated by bolting a semantic layer onto a system that wasn't designed for it

AIP: Why Enterprise AI is Finally Working - and Why Palantir is the Reason

In June 2023, Palantir launched AIP. At the time, the world was flooded with AI tools: ChatGPT, Bard, Copilot, and a hundred startups all offering AI that could generate text, answer questions, and write code. Every large company in the world was experimenting. Almost none of them were deploying AI in production in a way that actually changed how they operated.

The reason was always the same: the AI could chat, but it couldn't do anything. It had no access to real operational data. It had no understanding of business context. It had no permission to take actions. It couldn't be audited. It hallucinated. For a hospital, a factory, or a military unit, a chatbot that might be wrong is worse than no chatbot at all.

AIP solved this because the Ontology already existed. An AI agent deployed on AIP does not read documentation or browse the internet - it queries the Ontology. It knows what a Purchase Order is in your company, how it relates to your specific suppliers, what the current status of each one is, and exactly what actions it is authorised to take with them. It is not a generic intern given access to your files. It is a deeply briefed specialist who has read every internal document, knows every system, and has a clearly defined set of things they are and are not allowed to do.

Why It Can Still Be Worth Owning at $129

The case for buying at current prices is more demanding - it requires you to believe specific things about the next three to five years:

Claim 1: The US commercial growth rate will sustain at 100%+ for another two to three years. The evidence for this is the AIP pipeline: US commercial remaining deal value grew 112% year-on-year to $4.92B. This is not signed revenue - it is the pipeline of future contracted deals. At 75% bootcamp conversion and an accelerating AIP adoption curve, the pipeline supports continued triple-digit growth.

Claim 2: Net Dollar Retention of 150% means growth compounds from within. Existing customers are expanding their Palantir spend by 50% per year, on average. Even if new customer acquisition slowed dramatically, the installed base alone would continue to grow revenue at 50% annually. This is structurally different from a company that needs to keep acquiring new customers to sustain growth.

Claim 3: The Ontology moat means this is durable, not cyclical. Unlike advertising spend (ZETA) or loan originations (SOFI), an enterprise that has built its operational infrastructure on the Palantir Ontology does not stop spending when the economy slows. They cannot - the Ontology is how they operate. This creates a degree of revenue durability that most growth companies at this stage do not have.

Claim 4: Multiple compression will be gradual, not sudden. At 71% revenue growth, the stock can absorb valuation compression from 40x sales to 20x sales and still appreciate if the revenue itself doubles. The question is whether the growth sustains long enough for the company to “grow into” its valuation. At 85% growth, Palantir is growing into its valuation faster than almost any company in history.

The honest bottom line on valuation: Palantir at $129 is not suitable for the risk-averse investor. It is priced for near-perfection. A single guidance miss, a quarter of decelerating US commercial growth, or a macro-driven multiple compression could produce a 30-40% drawdown even if the business remains fundamentally sound. The correct frame for this stock is: small position, long time horizon, high conviction in the Ontology moat, and a stomach for extreme volatility. The people who made 10x on PLTR between 2022 and 2024 held through two periods of 50%+ drawdown. That is the type of ownership this stock requires.

Risks

Extreme valuation Insider selling International lag Ethical / political exposure Stock-based compensation dilution US commercial deceleration risk

Valuation leaves no room for error. At ~41x forward revenue and ~100x forward earnings, any deceleration in US commercial growth will be met with a sharp share price decline. The market has priced in perfection, and perfection is an extremely high bar to maintain for years at a time.

Insider selling is heavy and consistent. Alex Karp, Shyam Sankar, and Stephen Cohen have all sold significant blocks of shares on a regular basis through 10b5-1 plans. While these are pre-arranged and legal, the pattern of consistent insider selling at elevated prices is a signal that founders view the current valuation as attractive to sell at.

Stock-based compensation is substantial. SBC of $201.6M in Q1 2026 alone meaningfully dilutes shareholders. Adjusted metrics (which exclude SBC) look far better than GAAP metrics in part because of this. Real profitability, while growing, is lower than adjusted figures suggest.

Ethical and political risk is structural. Palantir's deep entanglement with US defence, intelligence, and immigration enforcement creates political exposure that commercial software companies do not face. A change in US administration, a high-profile controversy around contract use, or an international regulatory challenge could create headline risk that affects stock price regardless of fundamentals.

International commercial is growing slowly. At 38% growth vs 133% US, international is not yet participating in the AIP revolution at the same rate. If the gap widens rather than narrows, the total addressable market case weakens.

Palantir is not a surveillance company. It is not a meme stock. It is not a government contractor hedging its bets in commercial. The Ontology is the moat. It took 20 years to build and cannot be replicated by throwing money at the problem. Once an enterprise's operations are running on the Ontology, they are not leaving.

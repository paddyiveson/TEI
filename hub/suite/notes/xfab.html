Start Here: What Is X-FAB, Actually?

X-FAB Silicon Foundries SE (Euronext Paris: XFAB) is a pure-play specialty semiconductor foundry: it manufactures chips designed by other companies, on its own production lines, for a fee. It does not design its own branded chips and does not sell anything under its own name to end consumers. Its customers are "fabless" chip designers – companies like Melexis, ELMOS, and dozens of smaller automotive and industrial chipmakers – who hand X-FAB a design and get back finished silicon wafers.

The company traces back to the 1992 privatization of Kombinat Mikroelektronik Erfurt, an East German state semiconductor combine, and is domiciled as a Belgian European Company (SE) headquartered in Tessenderlo, Belgium, with its main operational hub in Erfurt, Germany. It listed on Euronext Paris in 2017 (ticker XFAB, ISIN BE0974310428) – not Brussels, despite the Belgian domicile, and not a US exchange. Roughly 61% of shares remain held by two founder-linked holding companies (Elex NV and Sensinnovat BV) and Sarawak Technology Holdings, the investment arm of the Malaysian state of Sarawak, which co-owns X-FAB's Kuching fab; the free float is about 39%, which is part of why liquidity and analyst coverage are both thin.

In Plain English

This is not TSMC. Not even close, and that's the point.

When people hear "semiconductor foundry" they think of TSMC or Samsung racing to shrink transistors below 2 nanometers for the latest smartphone or AI chip. X-FAB does the opposite: it runs mature, specialized process technologies – mostly 130nm to 800nm, generations behind the leading edge – because its customers don't need speed, they need chips that can survive a car engine bay, sense a magnetic field precisely, or run for a decade in an implanted medical device. Being behind the leading edge isn't a weakness here; it's the business model. Confusing X-FAB with a TSMC-type "digital logic" foundry is the single easiest way to misjudge this stock.

In Plain English

What do BCD, SOI and MEMS actually mean?

BCD (Bipolar-CMOS-DMOS) is a process family that lets a single chip both handle high voltage/power and do precise analog sensing – the combination automotive and industrial electronics need constantly. SOI (silicon-on-insulator) is a wafer construction technique that improves how chips handle high voltage and radiation, common in automotive and aerospace parts. MEMS (micro-electromechanical systems) are tiny mechanical structures – sensors, mirrors, microfluidic channels – etched directly into silicon, used in things like airbag accelerometers and blood-analysis chips. X-FAB's whole competitive position rests on being genuinely good at all three at once, which very few foundries bother to be, because it's a much smaller market than digital logic.

The Investment Thesis

X-FAB's case is a cyclical-recovery-plus-structural-niche story, not a growth story in the conventional sense. Three things are true at once:

1. A genuine, defensible niche

Very few foundries want to be excellent at low-volume, highly customized analog/mixed-signal manufacturing across BCD, SOI, MEMS and silicon carbide (SiC) simultaneously – the economics don't scale the way leading-edge digital logic does, and switching a qualified automotive or medical design to a new foundry is slow and expensive for the customer. That switching friction is X-FAB's moat: once a customer's chip is qualified on an X-FAB process, it tends to stay there for the life of that product, often five to ten years.

2. Automotive exposure, both blessing and curse

Automotive is roughly 58% of revenue, and the sector has been in a two-year inventory correction. That's precisely why the stock is where it is: automotive revenue was down 19% year-over-year in Q2 2026. But the same concentration means X-FAB is a leveraged play on the automotive semiconductor cycle turning – and Q2 2026 book-to-bill for the company overall moved back above 1.0 for the first time since Q2 2024, with automotive bookings growing sequentially for a second straight quarter.

3. Capacity built ahead of demand, funded partly by European industrial policy

Fab utilization sits around 60%, well below management's 85-90% target operating range – meaning X-FAB has real operating leverage on the table if volumes recover without needing much incremental capex. At the same time, it is investing through the cycle: a new "Fab4Micro" cleanroom in Erfurt for microsystems and photonics, backed by roughly €127.4M in EU Chips Act and German federal/state funding, under construction through mid-2028.

The Automotive Semiconductor Cycle: Where Are We?

Automotive chip demand fell hard through 2023-2024 as auto OEMs and their tier-1 suppliers worked through semiconductor inventories they had over-ordered during the 2021-2022 shortage. Broader analog-sector data in mid-2026 points to that correction finally clearing: distributor inventory days are back inside historical norms industry-wide, bookings are described as the highest in three years for several large analog chipmakers, and NXP – a bellwether for automotive semis – has guided for accelerating growth through the rest of 2026 while explicitly calling automotive its "slowest-recovering" end market, i.e. still catching up, not yet fully caught up.

X-FAB's own numbers are consistent with a market that has bottomed but not yet rebuilt: Q2 2026 automotive revenue was still down 19% year-over-year, but bookings rose sequentially for a second consecutive quarter and CEO Damien Macq characterized the first half of 2026 as the likely bottom. That combination – still-negative year-over-year comparisons alongside improving sequential bookings – is exactly what an inflection point looks like from the inside, but it is not yet a confirmed recovery. One or two more quarters of sequential improvement, ideally with automotive revenue turning positive year-over-year, would be the real confirmation.

Caution on causality: X-FAB is a foundry, not a chip brand, so its automotive recovery lags its fabless customers' own recoveries by a design-and-qualification cycle. If Melexis, ELMOS and similar customers are seeing stronger bookings today, that typically shows up in X-FAB's wafer orders with a delay of one to a few quarters – useful context for reading "coincident" indicators like NXP's guidance against X-FAB's own numbers.

Financial Position: Trough Margins, Manageable but Real Leverage

X-FAB reports in US dollars despite its Euronext-Paris/EUR listing (a legacy of its global customer and cost base); figures below are converted to euros at approximately €1 = $1.14, the exchange rate the company itself uses in its Q3 2026 guidance, and should be read as approximate.

Q2 2026 revenue was €175.3M ($199.8M), down 7% year-over-year but up 2% sequentially – within the company's own $190-200M guidance range. Gross margin was thin at 15.3%, EBITDA margin was 16.8% (€29.5M), and the company posted a net loss of €13.0M ($14.8M), versus a small net profit a year earlier. For H1 2026 as a whole: revenue of €346.8M ($395.4M), EBITDA margin 17.1%, and a net loss of €12.2M ($13.9M) against a €10.4M ($11.8M) net profit in H1 2025. This is a business earning trough-cycle margins on falling volumes – not yet losing money at the operating level, but not comfortably profitable either.

Valuation and Peer Context — With a Coverage Caveat

Analyst coverage here is genuinely thin: 7 sell-side analysts, per MarketScreener, carry a consensus "Outperform" rating with an average target near €7.80–7.90 (roughly 10% above the current price) – but individual targets range from about €4.35 to €12.90, an unusually wide spread for a 7-analyst consensus. Treat this as a loose directional read, not a tight consensus the way you'd get on a large-cap; at this coverage level, one analyst dropping or initiating coverage can move the "consensus" meaningfully.

Trailing P/E is not a useful metric right now – FY2025 net income was thin (€26.4M) and H1 2026 was a net loss, so reported P/E figures swing wildly and forward estimates matter far more than trailing ones. On sales, X-FAB trades around 1.4x trailing twelve-month revenue and roughly 1.8x enterprise value/revenue; enterprise value/EBITDA on an annualized H1 2026 run-rate is roughly 10x – a rough estimate, not a reported figure, since X-FAB doesn't guide full-year EBITDA.

Near-Term Catalysts

Book-to-bill back above 1.0x (Q2 2026): First time since Q2 2024 – the clearest quantitative signal yet that order intake has turned, even before revenue confirms it.

€127.4M EU Chips Act award (June 2026): Funding secured for the Fab4Micro microsystems/photonics expansion in Erfurt, reducing the capital burden of that build-out.

New CEO installed (Damien Macq, Feb 2026): Internal promotion from COO with an operations/capacity-expansion background, succeeding long-tenured Rudi De Winter – continuity rather than a strategic reset, confirmed at the April 2026 AGM.

Q3 2026 results (expected late Oct 2026): Guided at €171-180M revenue, 17-20% EBITDA margin. A second straight quarter of sequential automotive bookings growth, or automotive revenue turning positive year-over-year, would be the strongest confirmation yet that the cycle has turned.

Fab utilization climbing off the ~60% floor: Every point of utilization gained toward the 85-90% target drops disproportionately to EBITDA margin, since most of the fixed cost base is already in place.

Fab4Micro first production (targeted end of 2028): A multi-year catalyst, but the milestone that would validate the medical/microsystems diversification thesis in hard revenue terms.

Silicon carbide (SiC) and data center design wins: Management has flagged early data-center-adjacent design wins in the industrial segment as a longer-term opportunity (management has cited a multi-year revenue potential in the hundreds of millions, though this is an early-stage, unconfirmed pipeline, not booked business) – worth tracking but not yet a number to underwrite.

Key Risks

~58% automotive concentration 2.3x net debt/EBITDA ~60% fab utilization Thin analyst coverage ~39% free float / low liquidity New CEO, unproven track record

Automotive concentration cuts both ways. At ~58% of revenue, X-FAB's fortunes are tied tightly to one cyclical, currently-recovering end market. If the "bottom is in" read proves premature – a real possibility given short order-cycle times that management itself has flagged – utilization stays low, margins stay compressed, and net debt likely keeps growing.

The balance sheet has real leverage into an uncertain recovery. ~€274M net debt against trough-cycle EBITDA is manageable today but leaves less room for a slower-than-hoped recovery or for capex overruns on Fab4Micro, which runs through mid-2028.

Coverage and liquidity are thin, which cuts against conviction, not for it. Seven analysts and a ~39% free float mean price moves can be sharp on modest volume, and there isn't a deep bench of independent research to check management's own framing of the cycle against. The wide spread in analyst price targets (roughly €4.35 to €12.90) reflects real disagreement, not just rounding.

Leadership transition risk, even if low-drama. Damien Macq's promotion from COO looks like continuity rather than upheaval, but he has been CEO only since February 2026, through exactly the recovery inflection this thesis depends on being read and executed correctly.

Structural ceiling on the specialty-foundry model. Because X-FAB deliberately stays behind the leading edge, it will never participate in the highest-growth, highest-multiple parts of the semiconductor industry (advanced AI/logic). The upside case here is cyclical re-rating and steady niche diversification (medical, SiC, microsystems) – not a re-rating into a growth multiple.

What would move this from watchlist to portfolio consideration: (1) automotive revenue turning positive year-over-year, not just sequentially improving bookings; (2) fab utilization visibly climbing off the ~60% floor toward the 70s; (3) net debt stabilizing or declining for at least one full quarter as capex normalizes; (4) a widening of analyst coverage (more than 7 names) that would give more confidence in the current "Outperform" read. Without at least two of these, the current risk/reward does not justify adding it to the fund over existing holdings.

This is a small, thinly-traded, single-cycle-dependent European industrial stock. Position sizing, if ever added, should assume wide bid/ask spreads and sharp moves around each quarterly print – and should not assume the current "recovery" narrative is confirmed rather than merely plausible.

Bottom Line: A Real Niche, a Real Cycle, Not Yet a Confirmed Turn

X-FAB is a genuinely differentiated business – one of a handful of foundries in the world that does specialty analog, MEMS, and silicon carbide manufacturing well, with switching costs that make its customer relationships sticky once qualified. That's a real moat, and it's easy to mistake this company for something more exotic than it is (or dismiss it as a lesser TSMC) if you don't understand the foundry-vs-fabless distinction first.

Near term: the automotive semiconductor cycle looks like it has bottomed – book-to-bill above 1.0x for the first time in two years, sequential bookings growth for two straight quarters – but year-over-year automotive revenue is still falling, fab utilization is still ~30 points below target, and net debt is still growing. This is "the bottom looks to be in," not "the recovery is confirmed."

Long term: if automotive normalizes and medical/SiC/microsystems diversification keeps compounding off a small base, X-FAB has real operating leverage to re-rate off a depressed ~1.4x sales multiple. If the recovery stalls, a leveraged, thinly-covered micro-cap in a cyclical industry has limited room for error.

TEI positioning: Watchlist - not a current Growth Fund holding. Not high-priority, given how far this sits from being investable in size. If added, this would sit at Tail tier within the Growth sleeve: the niche and the emerging recovery signals are real, but automotive concentration, real leverage, ~60% utilization, and unusually thin/wide-dispersion analyst coverage argue against anything larger than a small starter position, and TEI capital is currently better deployed in existing higher-conviction holdings regardless. This is a capital-allocation call, not a rejection of the thesis.

The cost of waiting

Money invested earlier has longer to grow. This calculator compares starting a monthly saving now with starting after a wait of one to five years, shows the difference by retirement in today's money, and works out what it would take to catch up.

Starting later rarely feels expensive. The monthly amount is the same and a year goes by quickly. The cost shows up decades later, because the money from the missed months never gets the time to grow.

How the numbers work

01

The missed year is small. What it grows into is not.

Twelve payments of £300 come to £3,600, or about £3,650 to £3,750 with a year's growth at any of these rates. What changes with the rate is what that sum grows into: by 60, from £4,140 at 3% to £24,584 at 9%, in today's money. A higher growth assumption also means more risk, with bigger rises and falls along the way.

02

Catching up later costs more than the wait.

After a one-year wait, £19.24 a month on top for the next 31 years finishes level, about £7,160 in all against the £3,600 not paid. Each pound paid later has less time to grow. A lump sum of £3,682 at the end of the wait closes the gap at once, because it puts back the missed year with its growth.

03

Today's money keeps the pots comparable.

Prices rise over time, so the pots are shown in today's money: what they would buy now, with inflation at 2.5% a year. In pounds at the time, the £125,800 pot would read about £277,200 at 60.

What this does not say. It does not say that any of these growth rates will happen, or that investing now is the right step for everyone. Real returns vary from year to year and can be negative for long stretches. Fees and tax would lower every figure, and inflation could run above or below 2.5%. The payments stay level here; raising them over time would change both pots. It leaves out other calls on the same money, such as clearing expensive debt or keeping an emergency fund, and it leaves out pension tax relief, which would add to a pension saver's pot.

Education, not personal financial advice. The calculator shows how the mechanism works on the numbers entered; it is not a recommendation to save, invest or choose any growth rate.

Start Here: What Is a Compound Semiconductor Wafer, and Why Does IQE Matter?

IQE plc is a Cardiff-based company that grows ultra-thin, ultra-pure layers of crystal onto semiconductor wafers - a process called epitaxy. It does not make finished chips, and it does not sell to consumers. It sells the specialised raw material that other companies - chip designers, module makers, and ultimately the world's largest technology companies - turn into finished products. If you have ever unlocked a phone with your face, connected to 5G, or used an AI service running in a data centre, there is a reasonable chance a piece of that chain started life as an IQE wafer.

The Problem

Why Can't You Just Use Ordinary Silicon?

Almost every computer chip in the world - the processor in your laptop, the memory in your phone - is made from silicon. Silicon is abundant, cheap, and extraordinarily well understood after 60 years of industry investment. But silicon has a fundamental limitation: it is very good at switching electrical signals on and off, but it is genuinely bad at working with light. Silicon cannot efficiently emit light, and it struggles with certain high-frequency radio signals that modern wireless communication depends on.

This matters more than it might sound. Modern AI data centres move staggering volumes of data between chips, and increasingly that data needs to travel as pulses of light through fibre-optic connections rather than as electrical signals through copper wires, because light is faster, generates less heat, and can carry far more information over distance. Turning an electrical signal into light - and back again - requires a laser and a light detector. Silicon cannot make either of these well. You need a different family of materials entirely: compound semiconductors, built from combinations of elements like gallium, arsenic, indium, and phosphorus, which have the specific atomic properties needed to emit and detect light efficiently, or to handle radio frequencies silicon cannot.

Think of it this way: Silicon is like an incredibly skilled carpenter - brilliant at almost every job in the workshop, and available everywhere at low cost. But for certain jobs - anything involving light, or certain high-frequency radio work - you need a specialist glassblower instead, because the carpenter simply does not have the right tools for that material. Compound semiconductors are that specialist material. IQE is one of a small number of companies in the world that has spent decades mastering how to grow it reliably, at commercial scale.

The Craft

What Epitaxy Actually Is

Epitaxy is the process of growing a crystal, one atomic layer at a time, on top of a wafer substrate - using specialised machines (MOCVD and MBE reactors) that deposit vaporised source materials with atomic-level precision, inside vacuum chambers, at carefully controlled temperatures. The resulting layers are often just a few atoms thick, and the precision required is extraordinary: a tiny defect or an imperfect layer thickness can render an entire wafer commercially useless. This is not a business a well-funded competitor can simply walk into - the process knowledge, the equipment calibration, and the yield optimisation take years to build, which is exactly why the industry has consolidated around a small number of specialist suppliers globally, of which IQE is one of the largest and most diversified.

IQE grows several different compound semiconductor material families for different applications: gallium arsenide (GaAs) for radio-frequency chips and VCSEL lasers used in 3D face-sensing; gallium nitride (GaN) for power electronics and 5G infrastructure; and - the fastest-growing and most strategically important today - indium phosphide (InP), the material of choice for the optical components that move data as light inside AI data centres.

The Investment Thesis

Photonics Is Becoming IQE's Growth Engine - With Three Diversified Businesses Behind It

IQE's investment case has a clear centre of gravity: indium phosphide for AI data centre photonics is expected to become the company's largest single growth driver over the next five years, as the same copper-to-light interconnect shift powering the SIVE and LPKF theses elsewhere in this fund plays out across the optical transceiver market IQE has supplied for over two decades. But unlike a single-product pre-revenue story, IQE already has three separate, currently revenue-generating businesses supporting that core photonics growth engine while it scales - a genuinely diversified "picks and shovels" position across the compound semiconductor industry, not a single bet.

Key Relationships: MACOM, Tower Semiconductor, and the VCSEL Supply Chain

IQE's role as a component-level material supplier means its most important relationships are with other technology companies further up the value chain, rather than with end consumers directly. Several of these have been reshaped or strengthened over the past 12 months in ways that materially support the photonics growth thesis.

MACOM Technology Solutions

Strategic investor & long-term supply partner - April 2026

MACOM led IQE's transformational £81 million fundraise in April 2026, which came bundled with long-term strategic supply agreements positioning IQE as a key manufacturing partner for MACOM's high-performance semiconductor products. This is a structurally important relationship: MACOM is a significant supplier of RF, microwave, and photonic semiconductor solutions, and the agreement gives IQE a locked-in demand anchor alongside the balance sheet repair.

Tower Semiconductor

Multi-year InP epiwafer supply deal - announced June 15, 2026

A confirmed multi-year supply agreement for InP epiwafers supporting Tower's silicon photonics platforms, which serve 200Gb/s-per-lane pluggable transceivers, next-generation 400Gb/lane modulators, and optical circuit switches - infrastructure being built specifically for AI and hyperscale data centre networks.

Lumentum

Extended VCSEL supply agreement - December 2025

A long-standing relationship extended in late 2025, where IQE's VCSEL epitaxy expertise has advanced 3D sensing technologies across Lumentum's photonics product portfolio - key enablers for consumer face-sensing, automotive LiDAR, and in-cabin sensing applications. Lumentum is itself a major supplier into premium smartphone 3D-sensing (including Face ID-style systems), which is the closest verifiable link in IQE's supply chain to that end-market - an indirect but well-established relationship rather than a disclosed direct contract.

Undisclosed Global Technology Customer

$14M multi-year AI/data centre order - July 14, 2026

A multi-year production order worth $14 million from a "strategic global technology customer," manufactured at IQE's Newport foundry, supporting AI and data centre applications including high-performance storage technologies. IQE has stated it continues to engage with the same customer on future opportunities across multiple stages of the customer's data lifecycle. The customer has not been named publicly.

Reading the Apple/VCSEL connection honestly: IQE does not disclose a direct commercial relationship with Apple. What is confirmed is that IQE supplies VCSEL epiwafers to Lumentum, a major photonics component supplier that has long served the premium smartphone 3D-sensing market (the technology category that includes Face ID-style systems). This is a genuine and long-standing supply chain position - but it runs through Lumentum, not directly to any named handset maker. Investors should treat this as indirect exposure to the 3D-sensing consumer market via a confirmed intermediate customer, not as a disclosed Apple contract.

The Balance Sheet Reset: From Crisis Financing to Debt-Free

IQE's recent history includes a genuinely difficult period - multiple dilutive equity raises, a share price that fell to a 52-week low of just 4.66p, and a Strategic Review that at one point considered selling the entire company. Understanding how far the balance sheet has come in the past 18 months is essential to understanding why this is now framed as a core Growth Fund holding rather than a speculative turnaround bet.

FY2025 Segment Performance: The Mix Shift in Motion

FY2025 tells two very different stories depending on which segment you look at. Total group revenue fell 17.6% to £97.3 million, driven almost entirely by a 40% decline in the Wireless segment amid smartphone market softness. But Photonics grew 15% year-on-year in the same period, on accelerating AI and data centre demand for InP-based optical components. This is precisely the mix shift the investment thesis depends on: the cyclical, lower-growth Wireless business shrinking as a proportion of the group while the structurally growing, AI-linked Photonics business expands - a trend management expects to continue and accelerate through FY2026.

Risks: An Honest Accounting

Recent history of heavy losses Wireless segment cyclicality Strategic Review / takeover uncertainty Customer concentration in new AI orders Capacity utilisation dependent

This is a genuine turnaround story, not a clean growth compounder. IQE posted a net loss of approximately £36.7 million in FY2025, continuing a multi-year run of significant losses that necessitated repeated dilutive equity raises before the April 2026 MACOM-led fundraise. TTM gross margin has been reported as low as 1.4% at points over the past year, reflecting genuine operational strain during the downturn. The balance sheet repair is real and recent - but the underlying profitability recovery is still in its early stages, and investors should not mistake a debt-free balance sheet for a fully de-risked earnings profile.

Wireless remains a meaningful cyclical drag. A 40% decline in a single segment in one year is a significant swing, and while management frames this as smartphone market softness rather than structural loss of position, continued weakness here would offset photonics growth at the group level and could delay the mix-shift the thesis depends on.

The Strategic Review introduces genuine uncertainty. As recently as early 2026, IQE's Board was in discussions over non-binding offers for the sale of the Group as a whole, alongside separate bids for individual assets including a potential sale of the Taiwan operations. While the April 2026 fundraise and improving trading suggest the immediate crisis has passed, the possibility of a takeover, break-up, or further corporate restructuring remains live and could materially change the investment case in either direction - a full acquisition at a premium would be a positive outcome for shareholders, but a distressed or partial asset sale would not necessarily be.

New AI orders carry customer concentration risk at this stage. The $14M order and the Tower Semiconductor deal are genuinely positive, named, confirmed commercial relationships - but they represent early-stage concentration in a small number of large customers. IQE has stated it is not disclosing the identity of the $14M order customer, which limits investors' ability to independently assess that relationship's durability.

Profitability depends heavily on capacity utilisation. As a manufacturer with meaningful fixed costs in its wafer fabs, IQE's margin recovery is closely tied to how much of its installed capacity is running at any given time. FY2025 Adjusted EBITDA of £3.2M was itself described as impacted by underutilisation - continued volume growth is needed to drive the operating leverage management is guiding toward.

IQE's turnaround has real substance behind it - a genuinely important position in the InP supply chain for AI photonics, confirmed named customer wins, and a debt-free balance sheet as of April 2026. But this remains a company recovering from a multi-year period of heavy losses and dilution, with a cyclical Wireless segment still working through a downturn and an unresolved Strategic Review in the background. Treat this as a core holding built on a genuine structural thesis, not as a fully de-risked, mature compounder.

Bottom Line: The Raw Material Behind Three AI Infrastructure Theses

IQE occupies a genuinely differentiated position in this fund's AI photonics exposure: while Sivers and LPKF are both pre-volume bets on the copper-to-light transition in AI data centres, IQE is already shipping the indium phosphide material that underpins that entire transition, against confirmed, named commercial orders, today. The Tower Semiconductor deal and the July 2026 $14M order are not design-in pipeline - they are live, revenue-generating relationships.

The balance sheet transformation is the other half of the story. A company that spent much of 2023-2025 fighting for survival through dilutive raises and a Strategic Review that considered selling itself entirely has, as of April 2026, eliminated its bank debt entirely and brought in a strategic investor - MACOM Technology Solutions - with long-term supply commitments attached. That combination of de-risked balance sheet and accelerating, already-proven photonics demand is what elevates this from speculative turnaround to core Growth Fund holding.

The honest caveat: this is still a company recovering from real financial distress, with a cyclical Wireless segment currently working through a genuine downturn (-40% in FY2025) and an unresolved Strategic Review that could yet result in a full or partial sale of the Group. The photonics thesis is genuine and already validated by revenue - but the group-level numbers still carry real turnaround risk that a more mature compounder would not.

TEI positioning: Growth Fund core holding. The combination of a proven, revenue-generating position in the AI photonics supply chain, a freshly repaired balance sheet, and a valuation that still reflects turnaround-era scepticism (trading well below its 52-week high despite the balance sheet transformation) makes this a differentiated way to access the same structural AI infrastructure shift as Sivers and LPKF, with more near-term revenue proof behind it.

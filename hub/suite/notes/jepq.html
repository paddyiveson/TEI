What it is

A fund built to do one job well.

JEPQ is an actively managed exchange traded fund run by J.P. Morgan. It holds a portfolio of roughly 100 large US technology and growth companies built around the Nasdaq-100 index, names like Microsoft, Apple, NVIDIA, Alphabet and Micron. On top of that, the fund managers sell call options against the portfolio every month.

The fund is accessed here through its London Stock Exchange listing (LSE: JEPQ), the same underlying US strategy in a UCITS structure, tradeable in an ISA or SIPP through Trading 212 without needing a separate international brokerage account.

Selling a call option means giving someone else the right to buy your shares at a set price in the future, in exchange for an upfront payment called a premium. JEPQ collects that premium every month and pays almost all of it straight out to investors as income, on top of the ordinary dividends the underlying shares already pay.

The result is a fund that behaves like a milder, income-focused version of owning the Nasdaq-100 directly. You give up some of the upside if tech shares rally hard, in exchange for a large, steady monthly payment regardless of what the market does.

Role in the portfolio

Why it sits in the Foundation sleeve.

JEPQ earns its place in the Foundation sleeve for two separate reasons, not one.

First, broad exposure to the big US names in one holding. Buying Microsoft, Apple, NVIDIA, Alphabet and roughly 95 other Nasdaq-100-style companies individually would mean dozens of separate positions, each needing to be sized, monitored and rebalanced. JEPQ does that in a single line, giving the portfolio genuine exposure to the companies actually driving US markets without the admin of holding them all directly.

Second, the income. The Growth sleeve is built to compound over many years, concentrated in a small number of high-conviction names. That works, but it produces no income along the way and can sit through long, uncomfortable drawdowns. JEPQ converts part of the portfolio's exposure to big US tech into a monthly cash income, smoothing the ride and giving the portfolio something that pays out regardless of whether Growth names are up or down that month.

It is not chosen because we expect it to be the best performer. It is chosen because of the two jobs it does alongside everything else.

Yield and income profile

Where the ~12.6% actually comes from.

JEPQ's trailing twelve month yield has run at roughly 10-13%, paid out in equal monthly instalments rather than one annual dividend. It is important to understand that this yield is not primarily company dividends. The Nasdaq-100 names JEPQ holds pay very little in ordinary dividends. Almost all of the income comes from the option premiums collected each month.

Why the payout moves over time

Option premiums are larger when markets are more volatile and when interest rates are higher. That means JEPQ's monthly payment is not fixed, it will typically shrink somewhat in calmer markets or if interest rates fall, and grow larger during volatile periods. Since inception in 2022, monthly income has generally run between 0.8% and 1.2% of the fund's value.

The self-sustaining threshold
~£2,100
roughly 100 units, at current price and yield

There is a point where the monthly distribution alone becomes large enough to buy a full additional unit. Below that point, dividend reinvestment buys a fraction of a unit each month. Above it, every distribution mechanically adds a whole new unit to the holding, which then generates its own income the following month.

At that stage the position is compounding on its own each month, without needing any further contribution. Below roughly £2,100 invested, reinvestment still works, it simply builds toward a fraction of a unit until the balance crosses the threshold. Since the yield itself moves with market conditions, this figure will drift over time, but the mechanic behind it does not change.

The mechanics, plainly

Own ~100 Nasdaq-100-style stocks, sell monthly call options against roughly 20-75% of the portfolio's value, collect the premium, pay it out. Top 10 holdings typically make up around 40% of the fund. Holdings are reviewed and rebalanced regularly to track the underlying index.

Risk profile

What to actually understand before holding this.

  • Capped upside. If Nasdaq-100 names rally sharply, JEPQ will lag well behind, since gains above the option strike prices are given away to whoever bought the calls.
  • Still equity risk. The high yield does not protect the underlying share price. In a genuine tech sell-off, JEPQ's unit price falls too, just as it would for direct ownership.
  • Yield is not guaranteed. The monthly payment moves with volatility and rates. A calmer market or falling rates can meaningfully reduce the income paid out.
  • Tax treatment of distributions can be more complex than a simple dividend, since option premium income is often taxed differently. Held inside an ISA or SIPP this is not a practical concern.

The role, in one line: JEPQ is not held for growth. It is held so that part of the portfolio pays you every month, regardless of what the Growth sleeve is doing, in exchange for giving up some of the upside if tech shares run hard.

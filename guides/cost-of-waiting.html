The cost of waiting

What a wait of one to five years costs a monthly saving by retirement, and what it would take to catch up.

Starting later rarely feels expensive. The cost shows up decades later, because the missed months never get the time to grow.

How the numbers work

01

The missed year is small. What it grows into is not.

A year of £300 a month is £3,600. By 60 it grows into £4,140 at 3% or £24,584 at 9%, in today's money. Higher growth assumptions also mean bigger rises and falls.

02

Catching up later costs more than the wait.

£19.24 a month for 31 years adds up to about £7,160, against £3,600 not paid. Money paid later has less time to grow.

03

Today's money keeps the pots comparable.

The pots show what they would buy now. In pounds at the time, £125,800 would read about £277,200 at 60.

What this does not say. It does not say any of these growth rates will happen, or that investing now is right for everyone. Returns vary and can be negative for long stretches. Fees, tax, higher inflation or rising payments would change the numbers. It leaves out pension tax relief, and other calls on the money such as expensive debt or an emergency fund.

Education, not personal financial advice. The calculator shows how the mechanism works on the numbers entered; it is not a recommendation to save, invest or choose any growth rate.

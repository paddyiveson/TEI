Start Here: What Is Navitas Semiconductor?

Navitas Semiconductor (NASDAQ: NVTS) designs power semiconductor chips — the components that convert electricity from one voltage or form (AC or DC) to another, sitting inside every charger, power supply, and battery system ever built. Navitas doesn't make ordinary silicon power chips; it makes them out of gallium nitride (GaN) and, since a 2022 acquisition, silicon carbide (SiC) — two "wide bandgap" materials that switch electricity faster and waste far less of it as heat than 60-year-old silicon technology.

For most of its public life (Navitas listed via SPAC merger in October 2021), that technology showed up in one place consumers could actually hold: the small, cool GaN fast chargers sold by Anker, Xiaomi, and other phone-charger brands. Through 2025, management made a deliberate, painful decision to walk away from most of that low-margin mobile and consumer business and re-point the entire company at "high-power" markets — AI data centers, EV drivetrains, solar and grid infrastructure — where GaN and SiC solve a much bigger, much more expensive problem. The company calls this pivot "Navitas 2.0." Revenue fell by nearly half along the way. Then, in May 2025, Nvidia selected Navitas to help design the power architecture for its next-generation AI data centers, and the market began pricing Navitas as an AI infrastructure stock rather than a consumer-electronics component maker.

In Plain English

Why does an AI data center need a "power chip" company at all?

A single Nvidia AI server rack can now draw as much electricity as dozens of houses, and that power has to be converted — stepped down from the high-voltage grid, then down again, then again — before it reaches a chip running at just a few volts. Every one of those conversion steps wastes energy as heat and needs cooling, which is itself expensive at data-center scale. GaN and SiC chips convert power with meaningfully less waste than silicon, which is why hyperscalers building gigawatt-scale AI campuses are suddenly very interested in a company most people had only encountered inside a phone charger.

Why Nvidia, Specifically

The 800-volt rewrite of data center power

Nvidia's next-generation AI racks are expected to draw over 1 megawatt each — too much for the 54-volt power distribution used inside racks today, which becomes inefficient and requires impractically thick copper above roughly 200 kilowatts. Nvidia's answer is to convert grid power straight to 800 volts DC at the edge of the data center and carry it at that voltage all the way to the rack, cutting out several conversion stages. Navitas's GaN and SiC chips are part of the hardware Nvidia is using to prove that architecture out. It's a genuine technical need, not a marketing tie-up — but it's also a design collaboration, not (as far as has been disclosed) a locked-in supply contract with a dollar figure attached.

The Investment Thesis: A Real Technology Bet on an Unproven Ramp

Navitas's re-rating case rests on three things happening roughly together over the next 12-24 months. Each has real evidence behind it; none is fully de-risked yet.

1. The Nvidia collaboration turns into shipped, priced revenue

Nvidia named Navitas a collaborator on its 800V HVDC architecture in May 2025 and showcased Navitas hardware — an 800V-to-6V power delivery board built around Navitas's GaNFast and GeneSiC parts — at its MGX ecosystem partner event at Computex in May/June 2026. That is a genuine, high-profile design win from the industry's most important AI infrastructure company. What hasn't happened yet, at least not publicly: neither company has disclosed a purchase order, a volume commitment, or a dollar figure for what this is worth to Navitas. The stock has already re-rated by multiples on the announcement of collaboration; the next re-rating (in either direction) likely depends on whether that becomes disclosed, priced revenue.

2. The Navitas 2.0 pivot has to keep working

Management chose to shrink the legacy mobile/consumer business rather than milk it, on the view that low-margin phone-charger volume was crowding out the higher-value, higher-margin high-power opportunity. That bet is showing up in the numbers: full-year 2025 revenue fell 44.9% to $45.9M as mobile was deliberately wound down, before bottoming at $7.3M in Q4 2025 and recovering sequentially to $8.6M in Q1 2026 and $10.5M in Q2 2026 (+22% quarter-over-quarter). High-power markets (AI data center, grid/energy, EV, solar) grew more than 50% year-over-year in Q2 2026 and now represent the clear majority of revenue. The pivot is directionally working; it is also the reason the company just posted two of its worst revenue quarters in years.

3. Domestic manufacturing removes a supply-chain question mark

In November 2025, Navitas partnered with GlobalFoundries to develop and manufacture GaN technology at GF's Burlington, Vermont fab — explicitly framed around building a secure, U.S.-based supply chain for a technology historically produced mostly in Taiwan and China. Development began in early 2026; production is expected to start later in 2026. Until that ramps, Navitas's AI data center ambitions still depend partly on offshore fab capacity, which is a live risk for a company whose biggest customer conversation is with U.S. hyperscalers thinking about supply-chain resilience.

Navitas 1.0 vs. Navitas 2.0

This is effectively a company mid-transplant: an old, shrinking revenue base being deliberately swapped for a new, faster-growing but still much smaller one. Toggle between them below.

The Nvidia Collaboration: What It Is, and Isn't, Yet

In May 2025, Nvidia selected Navitas to collaborate on an 800V HVDC power architecture for next-generation AI data center racks designed to support 1 megawatt and beyond. A year later, at Nvidia's MGX ecosystem partner ceremony (May 29, 2026) and Computex 2026, Navitas showed the hardware that collaboration has produced so far.

What's been demonstrated: An 800V-to-6V power delivery board built around 16 of Navitas's GaNFast FETs (650V, 11 milliohms), targeting 97.5% peak efficiency at 1MHz switching and 2,100 W/in³ power density in a board roughly 20% thinner than a mobile phone. Alongside it, GeneSiC silicon carbide modules rated at 2,300V and 3,300V for grid-to-rack power delivery, and Generation 5 1200V SiC MOSFETs for three-phase supplies. Nvidia's stated efficiency case for the 800V architecture overall: up to 5% better end-to-end power efficiency, 45% less copper, and roughly 70% lower maintenance costs from fewer power-supply failures.

Strategically, this matters because power delivery, not compute, is becoming the binding constraint on how fast hyperscalers can build AI capacity. A company that can supply working silicon across the entire 13.8kV-grid-to-6kV-chip conversion chain — both GaN and SiC — is solving a problem Nvidia and its data-center partners genuinely have.

What's genuinely unclear: Neither Nvidia nor Navitas has disclosed a supply agreement, volume commitment, or revenue figure tied to this collaboration. "Selected to collaborate" and "featured in the MGX ecosystem" describe design and demonstration status, not a locked purchase order. Navitas's own results show total company revenue of $10.5M in Q2 2026 across all markets — whatever the Nvidia-related contribution is today, it is not yet large enough to be broken out, and the timeline for it becoming a material, disclosed revenue line has not been given by either company. Treat every dollar figure attached to "the Nvidia opportunity" in analyst commentary as an estimate, not a confirmed number.

Manufacturing and IP: The Moat Question

Two other 2025-2026 developments matter for whether Navitas can defend this position once the market gets more crowded.

Onshoring: the GlobalFoundries partnership

Announced November 2025, Navitas and GlobalFoundries are jointly developing and manufacturing GaN technology at GF's Burlington, Vermont facility, explicitly pitched around U.S. supply-chain security for AI datacenter and critical-power applications. Development started early 2026; production is guided for later in 2026. This is a real answer to a real question hyperscalers are asking their suppliers, but it is not yet running at volume.

Patents: a lawsuit as a moat signal

In August 2026, Navitas filed a patent infringement suit against Renesas Electronics, alleging Renesas's SuperGaN product line infringes four Navitas patents (part of a portfolio of 300+ patents issued or pending). It's a useful data point either way: Navitas is asserting real intellectual property built over a decade as a first-mover in commercial GaN, and the fact that Renesas — a much larger, better-capitalized Japanese semiconductor company — is competing directly for the same GaN sockets underlines how contested this market is about to become.

Financial Position: Well Funded, Still Losing Money on a Shrunken Base

Navitas ended Q2 2026 with $557.4M in cash, up dramatically from $221.0M at the end of Q1 2026 and $236.9M at the end of 2025 — a raise (via a $250M shelf registration and a share offering) that management framed as opportunistically strengthening the balance sheet while the stock was elevated on Nvidia-related enthusiasm. That gives Navitas a long runway relative to its non-GAAP burn rate of roughly $9-11M per quarter, but it came at the cost of real dilution: shares outstanding are up over 20% year-over-year to 261M.

Navitas's edge is focus and breadth-of-materials: it's a pure-play on GaN and SiC with IP spanning both, and it has the most visible public association with Nvidia's 800V push of any power-chip supplier. Its weakness is scale: Infineon, onsemi, and even Wolfspeed can outspend Navitas on R&D and offer customers a broader catalog and balance-sheet certainty a company this small can't match. If Nvidia's 800V architecture becomes an industry standard rather than an Nvidia-specific design, Navitas will be competing for those same sockets against companies many times its size.

Valuation: How Much Is Already Priced In?

NVTS trades around $12.97, for a market cap of roughly $3.39B against trailing-twelve-month revenue of just $36.5M — a price-to-sales ratio near 93x (about 55x on forward estimates). Enterprise value/sales sits near 78x. For comparison, profitable, diversified power-semiconductor peers typically trade in the low-to-mid single digits on the same metric. This is priced as a pure AI-infrastructure growth story, not as a semiconductor company with $10M quarters.

The stock's own history is the clearest evidence of how much sentiment, not fundamentals, has driven the move: NVTS has traded between $5.44 and $34.17 over the trailing year, a run-up of well over 5x from trough to peak on Nvidia-collaboration news, followed by a pullback of roughly two-thirds from the high back to the current price. That is a market that got very excited, then took a large chunk of that excitement back — and it is still deciding where fair value sits.

The Street is genuinely split. Of 8 analysts covering NVTS, ratings run from Strong Buy to Strong Sell (1 Strong Buy, 1 Buy, 5 Hold, 1 Sell, 1 Strong Sell), with price targets ranging from $8 to $21 against a $14.08 average — barely above today's price. One widely-cited bull model requires roughly 52% compound annual revenue growth through 2029 (to about $143M) just to reach a fair value estimate implying modest (~13%) upside from here. Short interest sits near 15% of the float, among the higher levels for a stock this size, which tells you a meaningful slice of the market is betting the Nvidia story doesn't convert to revenue fast enough to justify today's multiple.

Near-Term Catalysts

NVIDIA MGX showcase (May-June 2026): Navitas's 800V-to-6V power delivery board and SiC modules were demonstrated at Nvidia's Partner Ceremony and Computex — the most concrete public evidence of the collaboration to date.

Q2 2026 return to sequential growth: Revenue grew 22% quarter-over-quarter to $10.5M, with high-power markets up over 50% year-over-year — first clear evidence the Navitas 2.0 pivot is inflecting.

SPAC-era earnout fully settled (Q2 2026): The $203.1M non-cash overhang from the 2021 business combination is resolved, removing a recurring GAAP-earnings distraction.

Q3 2026 earnings (date TBD, guided $13.5M ± $0.5M): Would mark the first quarter of guided year-over-year revenue growth since the mobile wind-down began — a real test of whether the pivot has bottomed.

Any disclosed Nvidia volume commitment or revenue figure: The single biggest re-rating catalyst in either direction — nothing has been disclosed as of this note.

GlobalFoundries Burlington, VT production ramp (guided late 2026): First evidence of scaled, U.S.-based GaN manufacturing capacity actually running.

Renesas patent litigation developments: A win would reinforce Navitas's IP moat and could yield licensing revenue; a loss would weaken the differentiation case.

High-power mix crossing management's "more than one-third of sales by year-end" target: A concrete, self-set milestone for how fast the pivot is completing.

Key Risks

Pre-revenue-scale AI opportunity ~93x trailing sales 20%+ YoY share dilution Unconfirmed Nvidia $ contribution Offshore fab dependency until late-2026 15% short interest

The Nvidia relationship is a design collaboration, not a disclosed contract. Every figure attached to "the Nvidia opportunity" in analyst notes and financial media is an estimate. Design wins in semiconductors routinely take years to become high-volume, priced shipments, and can also be lost to a competing supplier before that happens.

The valuation already assumes a lot goes right. At ~93x trailing sales, NVTS is priced closer to a hyper-growth software company than a semiconductor supplier, on a revenue base that is still recovering from a self-inflicted, pivot-driven collapse. Any stumble in the Q3/Q4 2026 growth trajectory would likely compress that multiple sharply, as the June-to-August 2026 pullback from $34 to $13 already demonstrated the stock's willingness to do.

Dilution has been real and could continue. Shares outstanding are up over 20% year-over-year, split between the 2026 capital raise and the settlement of SPAC-era earnout shares. The company is well funded for now, but further raises tied to scaling manufacturing or funding growth should not be ruled out.

Competitive intensity is rising, not falling. Infineon, onsemi, and Wolfspeed are all larger, better-capitalized, and pursuing the same high-power GaN/SiC opportunity; Renesas is contesting Navitas's patents directly. If 800V data center architectures become an industry standard, Navitas will not be the only credible supplier competing for the sockets.

What would move this from watchlist to portfolio consideration: a disclosed Nvidia (or comparable hyperscaler) volume commitment or revenue figure; two consecutive quarters of confirmed year-over-year revenue growth validating the Navitas 2.0 pivot has bottomed; GlobalFoundries Burlington production actually shipping at scale; or the valuation multiple compressing meaningfully (toward the 20-30x sales range more typical of high-growth semiconductor names) without the growth thesis breaking. Without at least one of these, the current risk/reward does not justify adding it to the fund over existing holdings.

This is a binary-feeling, sentiment-driven stock trading on the promise of a relationship whose economics haven't been disclosed. Position sizing, if any, should assume the kind of swings the stock has already shown — a 5x+ run-up followed by a two-thirds pullback, inside about 15 months.

Bottom Line: The Technology Is Real. The Price Already Assumes It Wins.

Navitas has a genuine, differentiated position: one of very few companies building both GaN and SiC power chips in-house, with a public, technically credible role in Nvidia's 800V HVDC data center architecture and a domestic manufacturing partnership addressing the supply-chain question hyperscalers are actually asking. The Navitas 2.0 pivot away from low-margin mobile is showing early, real evidence of working — two straight quarters of sequential revenue growth after a punishing 2025.

Near term: Q3 2026 earnings and any disclosure of Nvidia-related volume or revenue are the two things that would confirm or unwind this thesis over the next one to two quarters. Neither has happened as of this note.

Long term: if the 800V architecture becomes an industry standard and Navitas holds its design-win position against much larger competitors, this could become a structurally important, profitable supplier to AI infrastructure. If Nvidia's own architecture choices shift, or a larger competitor displaces Navitas at scale, today's ~93x sales multiple has a long way to fall.

TEI positioning: Watchlist - not a current Growth Fund holding. If added, this would sit at Tail tier within the Growth sleeve — the underlying technology thesis is credible enough to earn a toehold position, but the combination of an unconfirmed revenue contribution from its headline catalyst, a still-loss-making core business, and a valuation that already prices in a great deal of success is too much unresolved risk for a Core or Anchor sizing. It stays on the watchlist rather than in the fund for the same reason as other names in this batch: TEI capital is currently better deployed in higher-conviction holdings already in the portfolio. This is a capital-allocation call, not a rejection of the thesis. Monitor: Q3 2026 earnings (date not yet announced), any disclosed Nvidia volume/revenue commitment, and the GlobalFoundries Burlington production ramp guided for late 2026 — whichever comes first should prompt a formal watchlist review.

Start Here: What Is Nebius and Why Does It Exist?

Nebius Group is a company that rents out the computing power that AI runs on. To understand why that matters - and why it is an extraordinary business to be in right now - you need to understand a fundamental constraint of the AI era: the chips that power artificial intelligence are extraordinarily scarce, extraordinarily expensive, and extraordinarily difficult to deploy at scale. Nebius is one of a small number of companies that has the expertise, the relationships, and the capital to do exactly that.

The Problem

Why Does Every AI Company Need to Rent Computing Power?

When you use ChatGPT, Gemini, or Claude, you are interacting with an AI model running on thousands of specialised computer chips called GPUs - Graphics Processing Units, originally designed for video games but now the backbone of artificial intelligence. Training a large AI model requires tens of thousands of these chips running simultaneously for weeks or months. Running that model for millions of users requires thousands more, permanently.

These chips - made almost exclusively by NVIDIA - cost between $30,000 and $100,000 each. A single modern AI data centre requires tens of thousands of them, costing billions of pounds to build and requiring enormous amounts of electricity to run. Most AI companies - even well-funded startups - cannot afford to buy and operate their own data centres. They rent the computing power they need instead, paying by the hour, from companies that have built the infrastructure.

Think of it this way: In the early days of the internet, every company that wanted a website had to buy and run its own server. Then Amazon built AWS and said: "We'll run the servers. You just pay for what you use." AWS became one of the most valuable businesses in history. The same shift is happening right now in AI computing - and Nebius is one of the companies being built to be the "AWS for AI."

Why Nebius Specifically?

What Makes Nebius Different from Amazon, Google, or Microsoft?

AWS, Google Cloud, and Microsoft Azure are the three dominant cloud providers. They offer AI computing, but they are general-purpose platforms built over decades for a wide variety of workloads. They are not optimised specifically for the dense, high-throughput GPU clusters that cutting-edge AI training and inference demands. They are also expensive, slower to adopt the very latest NVIDIA hardware, and less flexible for the specific networking and cooling requirements of modern AI clusters.

This has created space for a new category of company: the neocloud - a cloud provider built from scratch, specifically for AI, using the latest NVIDIA hardware, with purpose-built infrastructure and software designed for nothing else. Nebius is a neocloud. But it is a neocloud with one quality its competitors almost universally lack: a team that has done this before, at enormous scale.

Nebius was built by the engineers who created Yandex - Russia's equivalent of Google, at one point the fourth-largest search engine in the world. The same team that built hyperscale infrastructure for one of the world's major internet companies is now building hyperscale AI infrastructure for the world's AI companies. That institutional knowledge - how to build, cool, power, and operate data centres at scale, how to build software that runs efficiently on GPU clusters, how to attract and retain world-class engineering talent - is not something a startup can buy. It takes years to accumulate. Nebius arrived with it already in place.

Think of it this way: If you wanted to build a Formula 1 team from scratch today, you could spend a billion pounds on cars and staff and still struggle to compete. But if the entire Mercedes F1 engineering team left Mercedes and set up a new team together, they would be competitive from day one - because they carry the knowledge, the processes, and the working relationships. Nebius is that team.

The Origin Story

From Yandex to Nebius: How a Russian Tech Giant Became a Global AI Company

Until 2022, Yandex N.V. was one of the world's top technology companies - a Russian internet giant that dominated search, maps, ride-hailing, food delivery, and cloud services across Russia and several surrounding markets. It was listed on Nasdaq and widely considered the most sophisticated technology business in Eastern Europe.

Russia's invasion of Ukraine in February 2022 changed everything. International investors could not hold Russian assets. Western technology partnerships became legally impossible. The company's leadership - most of whom had moved to Europe years earlier - faced an impossible situation: a world-class technology business trapped behind geopolitical walls it had not built and did not want.

Over two years, the leadership team executed one of the most complex corporate restructurings in recent technology history. The Russian operations - search, maps, ride-hailing, everything tied to the domestic market - were sold to a consortium of Russian investors for $5.4 billion. What remained was everything internationally relevant: the AI cloud infrastructure, the autonomous driving division (Avride), the AI data labelling business (Toloka), the education technology platform (TripleTen), and a significant equity stake in ClickHouse, the fast-growing analytics database company.

The restructured company was redomiciled in Amsterdam, the Netherlands, renamed Nebius Group N.V., and relisted on Nasdaq on October 21, 2024 at $13.65 per share. With $2.95 billion in cash from the Yandex sale, it immediately began deploying capital into AI data centres. The stock has since traded above $200.

The NVIDIA Question

Why Did NVIDIA Invest $2 Billion in Nebius?

In early 2025, NVIDIA - the world's most valuable semiconductor company and the manufacturer of virtually every GPU used in AI - made a $2 billion equity investment directly into Nebius. This is not a commercial arrangement or a marketing partnership. NVIDIA put $2 billion of its own capital into Nebius stock.

To understand the significance: NVIDIA does not need to invest in its customers. Every AI company in the world buys NVIDIA chips already. NVIDIA choosing to invest $2 billion in Nebius specifically is a signal about what NVIDIA believes Nebius is capable of - and about how important Nebius's infrastructure is to NVIDIA's own long-term hardware roadmap. As part of the relationship, Nebius receives NVIDIA Exemplar Cloud status - meaning it gets early, priority access to NVIDIA's newest GPU generations before almost any other cloud provider. In a world where GPU supply is the single most constrained resource in the AI economy, being first in the queue for the newest hardware is a structural competitive advantage that money alone cannot buy.

Microsoft ($17.4B, five-year commitment) and Meta ($27B - $12B dedicated plus a $15B backstop) have also made major contracted commitments to Nebius. The demand risk for this business through 2028 is, uniquely among AI infrastructure companies, essentially zero. The challenge is purely one of building fast enough to serve it.

Nebius at a Glance: The Company Today

Nebius Group N.V. (NASDAQ: NBIS) is an Amsterdam-headquartered AI cloud company operating full-stack GPU-dense data centres across Europe, North America, and the Middle East. Its AI cloud platform provides GPU compute, storage, networking, and a software layer including developer tools and inference APIs - everything an AI company needs to train models and run them at scale, from a single source.

The company operates 16 data centres with more planned, anchored by its flagship Finnish facility which serves as the benchmark for its per-megawatt economics. It has secured over 4GW of contracted power capacity by year-end 2026 - the physical resource that ultimately limits how fast any data centre company can grow. Its 45% core EBITDA margin and 74% gross margin already rival mature software businesses, despite the company being barely two years old as a standalone entity.

The Yandex heritage is a feature, not a bug. The engineering team that built one of the world's top five internet companies by scale is now applying that institutional knowledge to build AI data centres. Nebius had NVIDIA Exemplar Cloud status on multiple GPU generations before most Western neoclouds had signed their first hyperscaler deal.

The ARR-per-Megawatt Framework

Analyst M.V. Cunha's revenue model builds from first principles. The Finnish data centre - Nebius's operational benchmark - generates approximately $10M ARR per connected megawatt at full capacity. At 800MW-1GW of connected power by year-end 2026 and 80% utilisation, that implies $6.4-8B in annualised revenue - precisely tracking management's $7-9B ARR guidance. The model is grounded, not speculative: it is based on one operational data centre that is already generating these economics, scaled linearly to planned capacity.

Risks: Brief and Honest

Customer concentration CapEx overhang Dilution risk GPU depreciation cycle Meta cloud competition

Customer concentration: Meta and Microsoft together account for ~80% of contracted backlog. Meta signalling it may compete in cloud services creates a relationship risk that must be monitored, even though the contractual commitments remain in place.

Capital intensity: $20-25B capex in a single year. Convertible debt rising toward $8B. Each equity raise reduces per-share value. The gap between cash deployed and cash generated requires continuous refinancing until the revenue ramp self-funds in 2027-2028.

GPU depreciation: NVIDIA hardware depreciates over 3-5 years, and each new generation can obsolete the previous one. Neocloud economics require continuous reinvestment at the capital frontier or installed base economics erode.

The $15B Meta backstop is the most watched number in Nebius's story. If it is fully converted to enterprise capacity at higher margins, as management expects, it is a material upside. If enterprise demand disappoints and Meta itself moves to fill that capacity, the utilisation thesis needs to be revised. Q2 2026 results on July 29 will be the first data point on enterprise pipeline conversion.

Bottom Line: The Purest Full-Stack AI Infrastructure Bet in Public Markets

Nebius is not a GPU rental company. It is a vertically integrated AI cloud business with $44B in contracted demand, $9.3B in cash, NVIDIA's direct equity backing, Nasdaq-100 membership, and a hidden portfolio of private companies worth approximately $7 billion that the market is not pricing in.

The engineering advantage is real. The team that built Yandex to hyperscale arrived in Western markets already knowing how to build, cool, power, and operate data centres at scale. No VC-backed neocloud startup has that institutional knowledge. The 45% core EBITDA margin and 74% gross margin - in a business that is barely two years old - are the proof.

The demand risk is essentially zero. $44B in contracted backlog from Microsoft, Meta, and NVIDIA means Nebius's revenue trajectory through 2028 is not dependent on winning new customers. It is dependent on building fast enough to serve the customers it already has. The Pennsylvania 1.2GW site and the >4GW contracted power portfolio give high visibility into that buildout.

The next catalyst is July 29. Q2 2026 earnings will deliver the first enterprise pipeline conversion data - the key question being whether the Meta backstop capacity is being absorbed by enterprise customers at higher margins as management expects. If yes, the revenue ramp re-accelerates. If not, the utilisation thesis requires reassessment.

TEI positioning: High-conviction AI infrastructure hold. The combination of Yandex-grade engineering, NVIDIA's explicit equity backing, Microsoft and Meta contracted demand, and an unpriced $7B sum-of-parts portfolio makes this the most structurally sound neocloud bet in public markets.

Start Here: What Is MercadoLibre?

MercadoLibre (NASDAQ: MELI) is Latin America's largest e-commerce and digital finance platform, often shorthanded as "Amazon plus PayPal for Latin America." Founded in Buenos Aires in 1999 by Marcos Galperin (still CEO), it now operates a marketplace, a payments and lending business called Mercado Pago, and a logistics network called Mercado Envios across 18 countries, with Brazil, Mexico, and Argentina as its three largest and most closely watched markets.

The three pieces are designed to reinforce each other: the marketplace generates transactions, Mercado Pago processes and finances them (both on- and increasingly off-platform, as a standalone digital wallet and acquiring business), and Mercado Envios gets the physical goods there fast enough to keep buyers coming back. Management calls this an "ecosystem" strategy, and the data backs it up - so-called "ecosystemic" users who touch both commerce and fintech generate multiples of the contribution profit of single-platform users.

In Plain English

Why did profit fall in a record-revenue quarter?

Revenue and profit don't have to move together, and this quarter they deliberately didn't. MercadoLibre cut seller commissions and offered discounts to buyers who pay with Brazil's Pix instant-payment rail, lowered the free-shipping threshold in Brazil, and poured money into growing its credit card book - all choices that trade near-term margin for market share and deeper customer engagement. Management has said plainly it prioritizes "long-term engagement, growth and scale over near-term profitability." That's a legitimate strategy if the growth sticks - but it only shows up as good investing in hindsight, once the payoff is visible in the numbers.

In Plain English

What is Mercado Credito, and why does its size matter?

Mercado Credito is MercadoLibre's consumer and merchant lending arm - credit cards, personal loans, and working-capital loans to sellers on the platform. It has grown into a $16.4 billion loan book, up 75% in a single year. A fast-growing loan book is a good sign when the loans perform well and a warning sign when they don't, because losses on bad loans show up on the income statement well after the loans were made. That's why asset-quality metrics (covered later in this note) matter as much as the growth rate itself.

The Investment Thesis: Three Engines, One Flywheel

MercadoLibre's Q2 2026 revenue of $10.2 billion split almost evenly between its two reporting segments: Commerce at roughly $5.8 billion (+50% YoY) and Mercado Pago (Fintech) at roughly $4.4 billion (+49% YoY). Both are growing at nearly identical rates, which is itself the thesis in miniature - this is no longer primarily an e-commerce company with a payments side business, but two large, comparably-sized businesses compounding off the same user base.

Marketplace: still gaining users, and buying deeper ones

Unique active buyers reached 89 million in Q2, up 26% YoY, on 795 million items sold (+44.5% YoY). Items per buyer in Brazil alone rose 19% YoY, and conversion improved 1.1 percentage points - management's read is that growth is coming from existing users buying more often as much as from new-user acquisition, which is generally a healthier growth mix than pure user acquisition.

Mercado Pago: the fintech arm is now nearly as big as commerce

Fintech monthly active users hit 88 million (+30% YoY), and 2.6 million new credit cards were issued in the quarter versus 1.6 million a year earlier. Mercado Pago increasingly functions as a standalone digital bank and payments network used off-platform as well as on it - the single biggest structural difference between MercadoLibre and a pure-play e-commerce competitor like Shopee or Amazon's Latin American operation.

Mercado Envios: the least-disclosed, and currently the most expensive, of the three

Logistics is where a meaningful share of this quarter's margin pressure originated: management confirmed that shipping costs "escalated faster than corresponding revenue," compounded by lower free-shipping thresholds in Brazil and rising energy costs the company chose to partially absorb rather than pass through. MercadoLibre does not break out granular operational metrics (delivery-speed percentages, fulfillment-center counts, parcel volumes) in a way that is independently verifiable from public sources - so while directionally it's clear logistics investment is a margin drag right now, the note flags this as a genuine data gap rather than presenting a specific figure as fact.

Financial Snapshot: The Trajectory Behind the Headline Number

Revenue has accelerated for four straight quarters even as net income has declined YoY for three straight quarters - a pattern that is either the signature of a company successfully buying market share while it still can, or an early sign that growth is being purchased at an unsustainable price. As of this note, the balance of evidence and analyst commentary leans toward the former, but it is not yet fully proven out.

Geographic Mix: Brazil and Mexico Carry Argentina

MercadoLibre's three largest markets are pulling in different directions this year, and the blended results depend heavily on Brazil and Mexico's growth outrunning Argentina's macro volatility.

Brazil remains the largest and most profitable market and the one absorbing the most deliberate margin compression right now - lower take rates and Pix-payment discounts were called out specifically as Q2 margin drags, alongside a confirmed $4.5 billion, 21%-larger 2026 Brazil investment plan.

Mexico is the clearest current growth engine, gaining market share even against a domestic tax reform and softer local macro conditions, backed by plans for roughly 18,000 new jobs concentrated there - a signal of how much strategic priority management is putting on the market.

Argentina is the geography most exposed to currency and policy risk under the Milei government's ongoing exchange-rate liberalization. Management has said peso devaluation "shrinks the size" of its historically high-margin Argentine operations in reported (USD) terms and dampens local consumer demand, even as underlying local-currency trade keeps growing. The company frames its marketplace as historically resilient through Argentine currency cycles, and Brazil/Mexico gains have "more than compensated" for Argentina drag so far - but a further sharp peso move remains a swing factor for reported (not necessarily underlying) results that isn't fully within management's control.

Mercado Credito: Growing Fast, Watching Closely

The credit book is the single fastest-growing, and highest-scrutiny, piece of the fintech engine. At $16.4 billion outstanding (+75% YoY), it is now large enough that its asset quality has a real effect on consolidated earnings - and it is growing roughly 1.5x faster than the company overall.

Reading the tension here: Delinquency ratios are genuinely good and management says it is deliberately shifting toward lower-risk borrowers even as it grows the book fast - a combination that, taken at face value, is reassuring. But a 75%-YoY-growing loan book that is simultaneously improving its risk mix is an unusual claim to fully verify from public disclosures alone, and negative NIMAL on the credit-card sub-book means that piece is currently a loss-leader for engagement rather than a profit center. This is a "trust but verify, one more quarter" item, not a red flag today.

Near-Term Catalysts

First-ever $10B revenue quarter (Q2 2026): Confirms scale, though it arrived alongside a third straight YoY net income decline.

Mexico investment and hiring push: ~18,000 planned new jobs and continued market-share gains despite a domestic tax reform headwind.

Brazil $4.5B 2026 investment plan (+21% YoY): Signals management conviction in the largest market even while compressing its near-term margin.

Q3 2026 earnings (date not yet announced): First real read on whether margin stabilizes sequentially or keeps compressing - the single most important near-term data point for this thesis.

Credit book asset-quality trend through year-end: Whether NPL ratios hold near current lows as the $16.4B book keeps growing at 75%+ YoY.

Argentina currency/policy path: Any further material peso move under the evolving exchange-rate framework would show up directly in reported (USD) results.

Competitive response cadence from Amazon/Shein/Temu/Shopee: Whether rivals escalate spending further (as Amazon did with its Nu Holdings tie-up) or the current pressure plateaus.

Key Risks

Three straight quarters of YoY profit decline No formal margin-recovery guidance 75%-YoY-growing credit book Argentina currency/macro exposure Amazon + Shein/Temu + Shopee, simultaneously Valuation still ~52x trailing earnings

The margin story is a bet on management's judgment, not yet a proven outcome. Cutting take rates, subsidizing shipping, and growing a credit book faster than the overall business are all defensible growth investments - but none of them come with a committed end-date or margin floor, and the market's post-earnings reaction (a 9% intraday drop) shows investors are not fully convinced yet either.

Credit risk is a slower-moving but real variable. Current NPL ratios are genuinely good, but a loan book growing at 75% YoY can outrun the seasoning needed to fully see delinquency in newer vintages - asset quality here deserves at least one more quarter of confirmation before being treated as fully de-risked.

Argentina is a real, if partially hedged, macro exposure. Currency and policy volatility there compresses reported results in a way management has only partial control over, even if Brazil and Mexico have offset it so far.

Competitive intensity is now three-fronted rather than one-fronted. Amazon's capital commitment, Shein/Temu's cross-border cost structure, and Shopee's budget positioning are each individually manageable pressures MercadoLibre has faced before in some form; facing meaningful pressure from all three at once, while simultaneously investing in fintech and credit, is a new combination for this company.

Valuation still prices in a lot of the good news. At ~52x trailing and ~46x forward earnings, shares are not priced as a distressed or out-of-favor name - a genuine margin-recovery disappointment would have real room to hurt the multiple as well as the earnings.

This is a high-quality operator making a deliberate, self-funded bet that growing faster now is worth more than protecting margin today - a bet that has generally paid off for MercadoLibre in past cycles, but one that is not yet confirmed in this cycle, against tougher and more numerous competitors than it has faced before.

What would move this from watchlist to portfolio consideration: (1) a sequential stabilization or improvement in operating margin at the Q3 2026 print, rather than continued compression; (2) confirmed asset-quality stability in the Mercado Credito book through at least one more full quarterly cycle as it approaches or exceeds $18-20B; (3) a pullback toward the lower half of the 52-week range ($1,495-$1,700ish) that would meaningfully improve the entry multiple without a change in the underlying thesis; or (4) explicit management commentary giving investors a timeline or floor for the current margin-investment cycle. Without at least one of these, the current risk/reward does not justify adding it to the fund over existing holdings.

Bottom Line: The Flywheel Is Real. The Bill Is Coming Due Later Than Expected.

MercadoLibre is not a broken growth story - unique buyers, GMV, TPV, and revenue are all compounding at rates most companies would envy, and management is making a coherent, explained choice to spend margin on share and engagement rather than protect it. That is precisely the kind of decision that looks smart two years later and unsettling in the quarter it happens.

Near term: the next one to two quarters of margin trajectory, and confirmation that the credit book's strong asset quality holds as it keeps growing fast, are the two things that would most change the picture from here.

Long term: if the fintech and logistics investments convert into a durable engagement advantage that Amazon, Shopee, and Shein/Temu can't easily replicate, MercadoLibre's current margin dip becomes a footnote in a much larger compounding story - which is the bull case most of Wall Street (0 Sell ratings among covering analysts) currently endorses.

TEI positioning: Watchlist - not a current Growth Fund holding. Not urgent, but worth revisiting each quarter. If added, this would sit at Core tier within the Growth Fund sleeve rather than Anchor or Tail: the business quality, scale, and multi-engine diversification are well above a typical Tail-tier speculative name, but the unresolved margin trajectory and un-seasoned credit growth argue against an Anchor-sized initial position. TEI capital is currently better deployed in higher-conviction ideas already held in the fund - this is a capital-allocation call, not a rejection of the thesis. Monitor: the Q3 2026 earnings print (date not yet announced), Mercado Credito NPL trends as the book crosses the $18-20B range, and any material Argentina currency move. A pullback into the lower half of the 52-week range would also prompt an earlier formal watchlist review, whichever comes first.

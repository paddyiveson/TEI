Nebius: Sizing up the Opportunity

Independent research and one Wall Street bank suggest Nebius could be much bigger by the end of 2027 than most forecasts assume. We have tested that research and explained it in plain English. Every forecast on this page is ARR: the yearly pace of revenue at a moment in time, not revenue booked across the year.

The one-minute version

  • Nebius rents out computing power to AI companies. Its income is how much capacity it has switched on, times the rent, which is the price a customer pays per megawatt (MW) each year.
  • Most forecasts expect ARR of about $14.7bn by the end of 2027. Research we have reviewed, built mostly on figures Nebius has confirmed, points to $19-22bn.
  • Everyone expects about $12bn of revenue across 2027. The disagreement is about how fast Nebius is running by December.
  • Faster growth costs more to build. How Nebius pays for it matters as much to shareholders as the growth itself.
  • If the consensus forecast is right, today's price looks about fair for the end of 2027. The opportunity lies in Nebius beating it.
The simple way to think about it

Nebius is a landlord for AI

Picture a landlord who builds blocks of rooms and rents them out. Nebius does the same with data centres. Every number on this page has a plain meaning once you see it this way.

The buildings
Data centres in Finland, the US and elsewhere
Number of rooms
Megawatts (MW) of power. More power means more AI chips running. 1GW is 1,000MW
The rent
The price paid per MW each year. Wherever this page says rent, it means this. Recent deals: $20-25m per MW
Long-let tenants on a discount
Microsoft and Meta. Huge, multi-year contracts at lower rent, about $11-12m per MW
Tenants paying full rent
Enterprise and AI companies, such as Revolut and Shopify, using Nebius's full cloud and software
Short stays at premium rates
Short contracts of up to six months, at $40-50m per MW
The yearly rent roll
ARR. This month's income multiplied by 12
Taking on partners to fund the build
Issuing new shares. Each existing owner gets a smaller slice
More terms explained
Active capacityRooms with tenants in. Power can be connected months before chips are installed and paying.

Vera RubinNvidia's next generation of AI chips. A newer, better room that may earn higher rent.

How it fits together

From rooms to share price in four steps

Every results report moves one of these steps. Knowing which one has changed tells you whether the news matters.

1

Rooms become rent

Active MW × rent = ARR

More rooms let, or higher rent per MW, means a bigger rent roll.

Risk: late buildings, falling rent, empty rooms.

2

December beats the year

Year-end ARR > year's revenue

Rooms open through the year, so 2027 revenue (about $12bn) is lower than December's rent roll.

Risk: most rooms opening late in the year.

3

Rent becomes profit

Revenue × margin = profit

Nebius keeps about 41p in each pound before chip wear and tear. Chips must be replaced every few years, so true profit is lower.

Risk: rising costs, price cuts.

4

Profit becomes share price

(ARR × multiple − debt) ÷ shares

Value the business, pay off the mortgage, split the rest between the owners.

Risk: lower multiples, more debt or shares.

Checking the research

Fact or opinion?

Confirmed by Nebius

  • $7-9bn ARR target for end of 2026, reaffirmed in August ($3bn at end of June)
  • Recent deals at $20-25m per MW, and enough demand to let all of 2027 on those terms
  • Short contracts at $40-50m per MW, and prices up about 20% from 1 October
  • Over 1GW of new capacity planned each year from 2027

Still opinion

  • That all 800MW of new 2027 rooms sits on top of the $7bn base. If they overlap, the total is too high
  • Over $40m per MW for Vera Rubin. No pricing published
  • Palantir adding to 2027 ARR. No figures given
  • 2027 guidance of $20bn or more, and $35bn ARR
The other side

What could go wrong

Buildings open late

Power, planning and chips all have to arrive on time. Microsoft's Vineland site had a planning hearing end without a decision. A delay there hits the $7bn base itself.

Rent falls

Today's rents reflect a shortage of AI computing. As everyone builds, that shortage may ease.

Paying for the build

Nebius spent $5.7bn in Q2 alone and has borrowed about $15bn through convertible bonds since mid-2025. Some of that may become new shares.

A few big tenants

Microsoft and Meta make up a large share of contracted rent. A change of plan at either would matter.

Rent roll is not profit

Chips wear out and must be replaced. Profit after that cost is a fraction of the rent roll.

Mood swings

Nebius fell about a third between June and August 2026 with no change to its targets.

Beyond the core

There is more to Nebius

This page focuses on the AI cloud. Nebius also owns stakes in other businesses. None of them are counted in any ARR figure here.

The other businesses and why they matter
Data softwareClickHouse

A fast-growing database company, reported at a $15bn valuation this year. Nebius has owned roughly a quarter of it.

Self-drivingAvride

Self-driving cars and delivery robots. Uber and Nebius committed up to $375m to it in October 2025.

AI training dataToloka

Prepares data used to train AI models. Took outside investment in 2025, with Nebius keeping a large stake.

EducationTripleTen

An online school retraining people for tech careers. A small part of the group.

Why this matters for shareholders. These stakes can be sold or partly sold to help pay for the build. Every pound raised that way is a pound not raised by issuing new shares, which eases the dilution risk.

TEI's take

How we read the evidence

The business evidence is strong and mostly confirmed. Nebius is letting rooms faster than it can build them, at rents that have risen through 2026. The research is built largely on numbers the company itself has given.

The disagreement is narrower than it looks. Bulls and consensus agree on 2027 revenue. They differ on how fast Nebius is running by December. That gap starts to close when Nebius gives its first 2027 guidance.

The real question for shareholders is funding. Faster growth means far more building. A great business can still be a disappointing investment if too many new shares are issued to pay for it. We are watching the share count as closely as the rent roll.

Our stance: the case for Nebius has strengthened this year, and the next results are an important test rather than a foregone conclusion. This is education, not a recommendation.

Bringing it together

Nebius is a landlord for AI. Its income is the number of rooms it has switched on, multiplied by the price each tenant pays per MW. Demand is not the problem. Nebius is letting rooms faster than it can build them, and the price per MW has risen through 2026.

Most forecasts expect ARR of about $14.7bn by the end of 2027. The research we have reviewed, built largely on figures Nebius has confirmed, points to $19-22bn. Today's share price already assumes a little more than the lower figure. The opportunity lies in Nebius proving the higher one without issuing too many new shares to pay for the build.

What we are looking for in Q3

The headline is 2027 guidance. We are looking for Nebius to guide to ARR of $20bn or more by the end of 2027. Alongside that, we want ARR above $4.7bn at the end of September, the $7-9bn 2026 target kept, new deals at $20m per MW or more, and a clear plan for funding the build.

What it could mean

Guidance of $20bn or more would suggest consensus is around a quarter too low, and analysts would likely raise their forecasts. The question would shift from whether the bullish research is right to how fast Nebius can build. Guidance at or below $15bn would suggest today's price already reflects the good news. Either way, the number of shares tells us how much of the growth reaches each shareholder.

Every pound you invest has a tax story. Understanding your wrappers is the single highest-leverage thing you can do before picking a single stock or fund.

      The Wrappers

      Know your options

      Each wrapper has different tax treatment, access rules, and ideal use cases. Most investors need more than one - but which ones, in which order, depends on your situation.

      I am:

      Investments

      ISA

      Stocks & Shares ISA

      The everyday investor's core wrapper

      All stages
      £20,000/ year - shared across all ISA types
      No CGT everNo income tax on dividendsFlexible access

      Your most versatile tax wrapper. Gains and income inside an ISA are completely free of tax - forever. No forms to fill, no limits on withdrawal. The foundation of any long-term investment strategy.

      More detail
      Tax treatment
      Capital gains
      Tax-free
      Dividends
      Tax-free
      Interest
      Tax-free
      Withdrawals
      Tax-free, no limit
      Key rules
      • £20,000 annual allowance - shared across all ISA types combined
      • Allowance does not carry forward - use it or lose it each tax year
      • ISA transfers between providers do not use your allowance
      • Must be UK resident, aged 18+
      Watch out for
      • Withdrawing and redepositing in a non-flexible ISA uses your allowance again
      • Not all platforms offer the same investment range
      Taxable Account

      General Investment Account

      No wrapper - use only after ISA & pension allowances

      Complex
      UnlimitedNo contribution limits
      No contribution limitCGT on gains above £3,000Income tax on dividends above £500

      Once your ISA and pension allowances are exhausted, a GIA gives you access to the full investment universe with no contribution cap. Every gain and dividend is potentially taxable - use Bed & ISA and your CGT allowance to gradually shelter gains over time.

      More detail
      Tax treatment
      Capital gains (basic rate)
      18% above £3,000 exempt
      Capital gains (higher rate)
      24% above £3,000 exempt
      Dividends (basic rate)
      8.75% above £500 allowance
      Dividends (higher rate)
      33.75% above £500 allowance
      Strategies to reduce tax
      • Bed & ISA: Sell in GIA, rebuy inside ISA - shelters future gains
      • CGT matching: Use your £3,000 annual exempt amount every year
      • Loss harvesting: Crystallise losses to offset gains in the same tax year
      • Spouse transfer: Transfer assets to a lower-rate-taxpaying spouse CGT-free
      ISA

      Junior ISA (JISA)

      Tax-free investing for a child's future

      Complex
      £9,000/ year - separate from the adult £20,000 allowance
      Separate to your ISA allowanceLocked until child turns 18

      A tax wrapper for a child's savings with its own £9,000 annual allowance, entirely separate from your own £20,000. Anyone can contribute. 18 years of compounding in a tax-free wrapper is extraordinarily powerful.

      More detail
      Key rules
      Who can open
      Parent or guardian
      Who can contribute
      Anyone
      Child's access
      Full control at 18
      Converts to
      Adult ISA at 18 automatically
      Why start early
      • £200/month from birth = ~£90,000 by age 18 at 7% growth
      • All gains and dividends completely tax-free
      • Doesn't affect your own ISA allowance
      ISA - Stocks & Shares

      Stocks & Shares Lifetime ISA

      25% bonus invested in markets - for long-term goals

      Growing +
      £4,000/ year - counts within your £20,000 ISA allowance
      25% bonus invested, not just savedTax-free growth on investmentsMarket risk - value can fall

      The same 25% government bonus as a Cash Lifetime ISA, but your money is invested in stocks, funds, or ETFs - giving it the potential to grow significantly over time. Ideal if your house purchase is 5+ years away, or if you're using the Lifetime ISA for retirement top-up alongside a pension. The bonus effectively gives you an instant 25% return before markets even move.

      More detail
      Eligibility
      Age to open
      18–39
      Age to contribute
      Up to 50
      Penalty-free withdrawal
      First home purchase
      Property up to £450,000
      Retirement
      From age 60
      Terminal illness
      Any time
      Why the S&S version often wins over time
      • 25% bonus is the same - but invested, it compounds over time
      • £4,000/yr for 10 years at 7% growth = ~£81,000 vs ~£56,000 in cash (illustrative)
      • Providers: Nutmeg, AJ Bell, Hargreaves Lansdown, Moneybox
      The penalty trap (same as Cash Lifetime ISA)
      • 25% charge on the full withdrawal amount for unauthorised withdrawals
      • Market falls can compound the penalty - plan your timeline carefully
      Advanced

      Bed & ISA / Carry Forward

      Techniques, not wrappers - but worth knowing

      Complex
      VariesTechniques applied to existing allowances
      Maximise existing allowancesRequires planning

      Two of the most powerful techniques for investors with existing GIA holdings or unused pension allowances. Bed & ISA gradually shelters taxable gains into your ISA. Pension carry forward unlocks up to three years of unused allowance for a single large contribution.

      More detail
      Bed & ISA
      • Sell assets in your GIA, immediately rebuy inside your ISA
      • Crystallises a gain or loss - plan around your £3,000 CGT exemption
      • Future growth is then sheltered from CGT and income tax forever
      • Can only shelter up to your remaining ISA allowance each tax year
      Pension carry forward
      • Unused annual pension allowance carries forward for 3 tax years
      • Must have been a member of a registered pension scheme in those years
      • Can contribute up to £180,000 in a single year if fully unused for 3 years
      • Still cannot exceed 100% of earnings in the contribution year

      Savings

      ISA

      Cash ISA

      Tax-free savings - not investing

      Starting out
      £20,000/ year - shared across all ISA types
      Interest tax-freeInstant access (usually)No investment growth

      A savings account with no tax on interest. Useful for your emergency fund or short-term goals where capital preservation matters more than growth. Not a vehicle for long-term wealth building - inflation will erode the real value over time.

      More detail
      When it makes sense
      • Emergency fund (3–6 months expenses)
      • Short-term savings goals (under 3 years)
      • Higher-rate taxpayers earning significant interest outside an ISA
      Upcoming change
      • From April 2027: under-65s capped at £12,000 in Cash ISAs specifically
      • Remaining £8,000 can still go into a S&S ISA
      PSA outside ISA
      Basic rate
      £1,000/yr tax-free
      Higher rate
      £500/yr tax-free
      Additional rate
      No allowance
      ISA - Cash

      Cash Lifetime ISA

      25% bonus on savings - best for short-term house deposit

      Starting out
      £4,000/ year - counts within your £20,000 ISA allowance
      25% bonus - up to £1,000/yr freeInterest paid on cash balancePenalty if withdrawn for other purposes

      Holds your Lifetime ISA as cash, earning interest. Best suited for people buying a home within a few years - capital is preserved while still attracting the 25% government bonus. Providers include Moneybox and Tembo. If your timeline is longer (5+ years), a Stocks & Shares Lifetime ISA will likely outperform over time.

      More detail
      Eligibility
      Age to open
      18–39
      Age to contribute
      Up to 50
      Penalty-free withdrawal
      First home purchase
      Property up to £450,000
      Retirement
      From age 60
      Terminal illness
      Any time
      The penalty trap
      • 25% charge on the full withdrawal amount
      • Means you lose some of your own money, not just the bonus
      • Example: £1,000 saved + £250 bonus = £1,250. Withdraw: receive £937.50
      Cash vs Stocks & Shares Lifetime ISA
      • Cash Lifetime ISA: capital preserved, earns interest - good for short timelines
      • Stocks & Shares Lifetime ISA: invested in markets - better for 5+ year horizons or retirement use
      • You can hold one of each type simultaneously

      Retirement

      Pension

      Workplace Pension

      Free employer contributions - always use it first

      All stages
      £60,000/ year - shared with SIPP contributions
      Employer contributions = free moneySalary sacrifice saves NI tooLimited fund choice

      If your employer matches contributions, this is the highest returning investment you will ever make. Always contribute at least enough to get the full employer match before putting money anywhere else. Salary sacrifice schemes also save National Insurance on top of income tax relief.

      More detail
      Salary sacrifice vs relief at source
      Salary sacrifice
      Contributions before tax + NI - full relief automatic
      Relief at source
      Basic rate automatic; higher rate must claim via Self Assessment
      Auto-enrolment minimums (2025/26)
      Total minimum
      8% of qualifying earnings
      Employer minimum
      3%
      Employee minimum
      5%
      Key considerations
      • Check your fund - default lifestyle funds often de-risk too early
      • Review your retirement date setting (e.g. NEST defaults can be wrong)
      • You can usually transfer out once you've left the employer
      Pension

      SIPP

      Self-Invested Personal Pension

      Growing +
      £60,000/ year or 100% of earnings (whichever lower)
      Tax relief on contributionsTax-free growthLocked until age 57

      Every pound you put in gets topped up by the government via tax relief. A basic rate taxpayer investing £800 gets £1,000 in their pension. Higher rate taxpayers can claim an additional 20% back via Self Assessment. The most tax-efficient wrapper for long-term wealth - but you can't touch it until 57.

      More detail
      Tax relief on contributions
      Basic rate (20%)
      Automatic - provider claims it
      Higher rate (40%)
      Claim extra 20% via Self Assessment
      Additional rate (45%)
      Claim extra 25% via Self Assessment
      At retirement
      Tax-free lump sum
      25% of pot (up to £268,275)
      Remainder
      Taxed as income when drawn
      Minimum access age
      57 (rising from 55 in 2028)
      Key rules
      • Contributions limited to 100% of your UK earnings each year
      • Unused allowance can be carried forward up to 3 years
      • Tapered allowance applies if income exceeds £260,000
      • MPAA (£10,000) applies once you start drawing flexibly

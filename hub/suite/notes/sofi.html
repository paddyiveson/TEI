Start Here: What Is SoFi and Why Does It Exist?

SoFi Technologies started life in 2011 as a student loan refinancing company. The name stood for “Social Finance.” Fourteen years later it is something considerably more ambitious: a fully licensed national bank, a B2B financial infrastructure business, a crypto exchange, an investing platform, a credit card provider, and an insurance marketplace - all accessible through a single app on your phone, with one login and one unified view of your financial life.

The company is headquartered in San Francisco and led by CEO Anthony Noto, a former Goldman Sachs banker, NFL CFO, and Twitter COO who joined in 2018 and has driven the transformation from niche lender to full-service digital bank. In 2022, SoFi obtained a national bank charter from the US Office of the Comptroller of the Currency - a milestone that changed the economics of the business fundamentally, in ways that most investors still underappreciate.

The Problem

Why Is Managing Your Money Still So Complicated?

Think about what it takes to manage your finances today. You probably have a current account at a high street bank. A savings account somewhere else (because they offer a better rate). A stocks and shares ISA or investment account at a third provider. Your mortgage or personal loan at a fourth. Your credit card at a fifth. Insurance through a broker. A pension through your employer's chosen provider. Each has a separate app. Each has a separate login. None of them talk to each other. You can't see your full financial picture in one place. Moving money between them takes time and incurs fees. And your high street bank charges you little-to-nothing for holding your money, while offering you products that are rarely the best value available.

This fragmentation is not an accident - it is how the traditional banking industry was built, in an era before smartphones, before cloud computing, and before open banking. SoFi was built from scratch in the opposite direction: start with the app and build the bank around it, rather than take a legacy institution and try to retrofit a digital interface onto decades of old infrastructure.

Think of it this way: Traditional banking is like a high street with ten specialist shops - a money lender, a savings bank, a stockbroker, an insurance agent, a foreign exchange counter - each on a different street, each requiring a separate visit and membership card. SoFi is the supermarket that puts all of them under one roof, with a loyalty card that rewards you for spending across departments, and a staff who can see your entire shopping history to give you relevant offers.

The Bank Charter

Why Having a Banking Licence Changes Everything

This is the detail that separates SoFi from every other fintech app - and it's the one most people gloss over. Before January 2022, SoFi was a fintech company that had to borrow money at relatively high rates from other banks and the capital markets to fund the loans it made. This was expensive and limited its margins.

With a national bank charter, SoFi became SoFi Bank, N.A. - a fully regulated US bank, supervised by the OCC and the Federal Reserve. This gave it the legal right to take deposits directly from customers and use those deposits to fund its lending at a dramatically lower cost. Instead of borrowing at 5-6%, SoFi can fund its loans with deposits paying 1-4% - a structural cost advantage that compounds with every new member who opens a savings account.

Today, SoFi holds $40.2 billion in customer deposits, which fund over 90% of its lending liabilities. The net interest margin - the profit from lending at a higher rate than it pays depositors - is approximately 5.72%. This is the engine that turned SoFi from a loss-making fintech into a consistently profitable bank.

Think of it this way: Before the bank charter, SoFi was like a pub that had to buy all its beer from a middleman at full retail price, then sell it to customers at a small markup. After the charter, it got a brewery licence. Now it makes its own beer at cost price and keeps the full margin. The product is the same. The economics are completely different.

The Strategy

The Financial Services Productivity Loop: SoFi's Growth Engine

SoFi's growth strategy is built around a concept management calls the Financial Services Productivity Loop (FSPL). The logic is simple: attract a member with one product, then cross-sell additional products over time, increasing both the revenue per member and the member's loyalty to the platform.

Typically, a new SoFi member joins for either a high-yield savings account (currently offering market-leading rates) or a personal loan (for debt consolidation or a large purchase). Once they are in the app, they see the full product suite: investing, credit card, mortgage, student loan refinancing, insurance, crypto, personal finance management. The more products a member uses, the more data SoFi has on them, the better it can underwrite credit for them, and the lower the risk of default. More products also means more interchange revenue, more interest income, and more fee revenue from a single acquisition cost.

In Q1 2026, SoFi reported a cross-buy rate of 43% - meaning 43% of members who joined for one product had bought at least one other product. Super-scaled customers (those using three or more products) have materially lower churn, lower credit risk, and higher lifetime value than single-product members. The flywheel compounds with every new member added.

⚙️  Segment 03 - Technology Platform

Galileo APIs • Technisys Core Banking • The “AWS of Fintech”

The most strategically important segment long-term - and currently the most troubled. Galileo is a payment-processing and card-issuing API platform that other fintechs and banks plug into to power their own products. Robinhood, Revolut, and hundreds of other financial apps run on Galileo infrastructure. Galileo's long-term target: a $1 billion annual revenue engine, at the highest margins of any SoFi segment.

The Undervaluation Case: Best Quarter Ever, Stock at a Two-Year Low

The disconnect between SoFi's operational performance and its share price is the defining investment question of 2026. The business recorded its best results in company history. The stock trades at approximately half its January peak. Understanding why requires being honest about what is causing the discount - and whether each cause is structural or temporary.

Three Reasons the Stock Is Depressed

1. Muddy Waters Research short report (March 30, 2026). The well-known short-seller published a report calling SoFi a “financial engineering treadmill,” alleging that profitability metrics are managed through accounting choices and capital structure rather than genuine business improvement, and claiming charge-off rates are understated by nearly 3x. SoFi management responded aggressively, calling the report “factually inaccurate and misleading” and exploring legal action. Muddy Waters, which has “been sued a number of times and remains undefeated,” has not retracted. Short interest rose to 13.24% of the float (165 million shares), amplifying downward price pressure. Until the accounting allegations are definitively rebutted by future audited results - or by a regulatory clean bill of health - the cloud will persist. This is the single most important overhang on the stock.

2. Unchanged FY2026 guidance after a record Q1. When a company beats its numbers by as much as SoFi did in Q1 ($50M revenue beat, net income up 134%), investors expect the annual guidance to be raised. SoFi maintained its full-year 2026 guidance at $4.655B in adjusted revenue and $825M in net income rather than raising it. The stock fell 10% on the day. Management's explanation: macro uncertainty, specifically elevated interest rates and consumer credit risk, justified conservative guidance. But many investors interpreted the unchanged guidance as a sign that Q1 was front-loaded or that headwinds in Q2-Q4 would be material.

3. Galileo wound - Technology Platform decline. In Q4 2025, the Technology Platform segment reported a 23% year-over-year decline in total enabled accounts, primarily because Chime - one of Galileo's largest clients - migrated its core banking platform away from Galileo to an in-house solution. This is the one genuinely weak spot in SoFi's operating results and the reason the B2B technology infrastructure thesis that supports the premium multiple is under pressure. If Galileo is losing marquee clients, the path to becoming “the AWS of fintech” becomes harder to argue. Management has communicated that the Technology Platform is recovering, with Q1 2026 showing sequential improvement, but the account decline remains an open narrative question.

Why these are temporary, not structural: Muddy Waters allegations are addressed by continuing to report GAAP audited profits - each additional profitable quarter makes the “financial engineering” narrative harder to sustain. Unchanged guidance is conservative management behaviour in an uncertain rate environment, not evidence of deterioration (Q2 guidance suggested continued sequential growth). The Galileo wound is painful but not fatal - SoFi has 120M+ enabled accounts and continues signing new enterprise clients. None of these three factors touch the underlying consumer business, the deposit franchise, or the member growth story, all of which are accelerating.

The Valuation Disconnect in Numbers

At roughly $17.50 and a market cap of ~$22B, SoFi trades at approximately:

- 4.7x 2026 guided adjusted net revenue of $4.655B (for a business growing at 30%+ and guiding to $825M net income)

- 2.1x book value ($8.26 per share), which is cheaper than most regional US banks despite growing far faster

- A PEG ratio of approximately 0.70 - meaning the market is paying less than 1x for each unit of growth. A PEG below 1 conventionally signals undervaluation relative to earnings growth trajectory.

- 27x trailing earnings - elevated for a bank, but not for a 41%-revenue-growth technology platform with 10 consecutive profitable quarters

For context: Robinhood (HOOD) trades at approximately 20-25x revenue on a smaller base. JPMorgan, America's most valuable bank, trades at approximately 14-15x earnings on ~6% revenue growth. SoFi is growing at 7x the pace of JPMorgan and trades at a similar absolute earnings multiple. Cathie Wood's ARK invest purchased shares in June 2026. CEO Anthony Noto personally purchased $251,000 of shares on the open market - a strong insider confidence signal.

Muddy Waters resolution: Either SoFi wins a legal action, the Q2/Q3 audited results definitively rebut the accounting allegations, or an external review clears management. Any one of these lifts the short interest overhang that is amplifying every negative catalyst.

Galileo recovery: Net enterprise account growth returning to positive would restore the B2B infrastructure thesis. Management has guided for Technology Platform sequential improvement through 2026. Confirmation in Q2 reactivates the premium multiple case.

Risks: Clear-Eyed

Muddy Waters cloud Galileo wound Consumer credit risk Dilution history Macro / rate environment Guidance conservatism

Muddy Waters accounting allegations remain unresolved. The short report alleged near-3x understatement of charge-off rates, which if accurate would significantly impair the reported profitability picture. SoFi management has disputed this vigorously. Independent verification requires continued audited reporting. Until this is definitively put to rest, institutional investors will maintain a higher risk premium on the stock.

Consumer lending in a softening economy. SoFi's personal loan book is unsecured consumer credit - the category most vulnerable to rising unemployment or consumer stress. At $7.5B per quarter of personal loan originations, a meaningful deterioration in US employment could accelerate charge-offs beyond what current models assume. Q1 2026 net charge-offs of $201M (vs $169M a year earlier) already show modest pressure.

Galileo client concentration and platform risk. The Chime departure showed that even large B2B platform clients can migrate away. If other top Galileo clients - Robinhood, Revolut, or other major fintechs - were to follow Chime's example and build their own banking infrastructure, the Technology Platform thesis unravels significantly.

Dilution has been material. SoFi raised $3 billion in two share offerings during 2025 (July at $20.85, December at $27.50), adding approximately 126 million shares. December buyers are still underwater at current prices, creating persistent selling pressure as they rebalance or cut losses. Shares outstanding grew 13.65% year-over-year in 2025.

SoFi is emphatically not a “set and forget” position. It requires monitoring Galileo account trends, charge-off rate evolution, and the Muddy Waters overhang at every quarterly earnings print. The bull case is compelling at $17.50. The bear case - rising credit losses, confirmed accounting irregularities, Galileo further deteriorating - is not trivial. Position size accordingly and treat the Q2 earnings call as a decision point.

Bottom Line: The World's Best Quarter, the World's Most Sceptical Market

SoFi reported the best quarter in its history on April 29, 2026. Revenue up 41%. Net income up 134%. 10th consecutive GAAP profit. More new members than ever before. The stock fell 10% on the day. It is now trading at approximately half its January 2026 peak, while the underlying business has never been stronger.

The core argument for buying SOFI now rests on three facts: a banking franchise with $40.2 billion in deposits that funds loans at a structural cost advantage no non-bank fintech can match; a member flywheel adding 1 million new members per quarter with 43% cross-buy rates and compounding lifetime value; and a valuation at ~4.7x forward revenue with a 0.70 PEG ratio that the comparable fintech universe would price at 10-20x if the cloud were to lift.

The honest risk framing: The Muddy Waters report is serious, even if you believe management's denials. Galileo is genuinely wounded. The unchanged Q1 guidance raised legitimate questions about Q2-Q4 visibility. And the dilution from December's $27.50 offering means there are 50+ million shares sitting underwater that create persistent selling pressure. This is a stock that deserves careful position sizing, not a maximum conviction bet.

What to watch: Q2 earnings are the single most important near-term event. A guidance raise there, combined with Galileo account improvement and stable charge-off rates, resolves the three biggest investor concerns in one report. If all three deliver, the Muddy Waters narrative becomes very difficult to sustain against three consecutive quarters of accelerating, independently audited results.

TEI positioning: Long-term hold, presently seriously undervalued but out of favour. The combination of a national bank charter, FSPL flywheel, Galileo B2B optionality, S&P 500 candidacy, and accelerating profitability makes this a genuinely differentiated financial services franchise at a price that doesn't reflect it. Patience and quarterly vigilance are both required.

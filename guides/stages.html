The journey

Five stages, at your own pace

A defined sequence, from a first investment to a coordinated financial plan. Each stage builds on the one before.

Every topic builds on the one before it. Some people move through the Foundations in a single session; others need three. What never changes is that moving forward happens because the understanding is there, not because a calendar said so.

Stages 1 to 3 apply to everyone. Stage 4 matters more for anyone going beyond passive investing, and Stage 5 has no fixed end. The ideas behind all five are in TEI Principles.

Stage 1

The Foundations

Never invested. Starting from scratch.

From confused about ISAs to a first investment made with confidence. This stage covers the psychology of financial decisions, how the tax wrappers work and the order people often use them in, and how to choose a platform. By the end, the first investment is made, automated and understood.

By the end of the stage
  • First investment made, in an account chosen for its purpose
  • Regular monthly contribution set up and automated
  • Can explain what is owned and why, in plain English
  • Platform chosen and weighed against the alternatives
  • Knows the difference between saving and investing

The psychology of money

Most poor investment decisions are not caused by knowing too little. They are caused by how the brain reacts to money moving: buying when everyone is excited and selling when everyone is scared. The people who do well long-term have decided in advance what kind of investor they are going to be, so a bad week does not get to make a permanent decision.

Stage 2

Building the Base

First investment made. Now structuring the portfolio properly.

Knowing what is owned, why, and what it costs. This stage covers fund types, asset classes, spreading money across regions, and the core and satellite model for structuring a portfolio. By the end, the money sits in accounts that fit its purpose, at a cost that has been checked.

By the end of the stage
  • Money sits in accounts that fit its purpose
  • Understands every fund held, and why
  • Costs reviewed, including the ongoing charge on core holdings
  • Money spread across regions, not just the UK
  • Can explain the portfolio to someone else

How index funds actually work

Money is not invested in one thing. It is pooled and used to buy a tiny slice of every company in the index at once, in proportion to each company's size. Because bigger companies get bigger slices, a few giants can make up a large share of the index: diversified across hundreds of names, but still leaning on a few.

  • Market cap weighting: bigger companies mean a bigger slice
  • Concentration risk in cap-weighted indices
  • Rebalancing: how indices stay current
  • Tracking error: why a fund's return differs slightly from its index

Key terms: market cap weighting, concentration risk, rebalancing, tracking error

Stage 3

Growing with Confidence

Invested and structured. Now developing market understanding and conviction.

Understanding what drives markets, rather than being rattled by headlines. This stage covers how markets and cycles work, what moves interest rates and inflation, and the difference between volatility and permanent loss. By the end, a real drawdown can be sat through without panic.

By the end of the stage
  • Can describe where the market cycle seems to be, with reasons
  • Understands how interest rate decisions affect the portfolio
  • Pension reviewed: fund choice and contributions understood
  • Has stayed invested through a period of real volatility
  • Can tell short-term noise from fundamental change

Volatility and risk

The two words get used as if they meant the same thing, and mistaking one for the other leads to exactly the wrong conclusion. Once they are separated, a drawdown stops looking like a crisis and starts looking like what a bumpy but rising line does sometimes.

Stage 4

Investment Philosophy

Developing a personal approach. Going beyond passive.

A defensible approach that goes beyond passive investing and is genuinely one's own. This stage explores growth, value and thematic investing, and how to judge a company from its financial statements. By the end, there is a written philosophy and a clear reason behind every position.

By the end of the stage
  • A written investment philosophy or investment policy statement
  • A clear reason for every position held
  • At least one company judged from its financial statements
  • Core and satellite structure in place, with a rationale for each part

Why it works

Why a curriculum, not just content

There is no shortage of information. What is missing is a way to know what is not yet known, and the order it matters in.

Most people learning to invest piece it together from scattered videos, contradictory forum threads and whatever article showed up first. That leaves gaps nobody can see from the inside.

A curriculum fixes the order. Each stage assumes the one before it, and moving on depends on understanding, checked by a coach, rather than on time spent.

Before getting started

Common questions

1What if I already know some of this?

Sessions move quickly through anything already understood. Nobody sits through content they do not need, and the first conversation is where a starting point gets agreed.

2Do I have to go through every stage?

Most people do, because each stage builds on the last. Someone already investing with confidence who wants only the market understanding or the philosophy pieces can say so at the start.

3What if I never want to pick individual shares?

That is fine. Stages 1 to 3 apply to everyone. Stage 4 matters more for anyone going beyond passive investing, and a strong passive approach on its own is a legitimate and common outcome.

4How long does the whole thing take?

There is no fixed timeline. Stage 1 might be one session; Stage 5 carries on for as long as we work together. Some people reach Stage 3 in a few months, others take a year. What matters is that it is real, not rushed.

5Is this only for complete beginners?

No. Plenty of clients arrive already investing, sometimes for years, and start partway through. Finding that starting point comes first, rather than assuming everyone begins at zero.

What this does not say. This guide describes what each stage of our curriculum covers. It does not say which stage anyone is at, or what to invest in: that depends on goals, time horizon, existing savings and how much risk a person can live with. The topics are an outline, not the full material, and the order can change from person to person. Figures such as allowances are for the 2026/27 tax year and can change at any Budget. Investments can fall as well as rise.
